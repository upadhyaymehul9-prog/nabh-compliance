Why NABH Accreditation Matters More Than Ever for Indian Hospitals

MU
Dr. Mehul Upadhyay
NABH Quality Manager | Head of Medical Services
📅 June 9, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

A few years ago, I visited a mid-sized hospital in Gujarat. Clean facility. Experienced doctors. Decent patient footfall. On paper, everything looked fine.

But when I walked through their medication storage area, I found expired drugs still on the shelf. The nursing staff couldn't locate the last fire drill record. The patient complaint register had not been updated in four months.

No one was being careless intentionally. The systems simply weren't there.

That hospital is not an exception. It is the norm across hundreds of Indian hospitals today. This is exactly why NABH accreditation exists — and why it matters more in 2026 than it ever has before.

What is NABH Accreditation — In Simple Terms

NABH stands for National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers. It is India's official accreditation body for hospitals, set up under the Quality Council of India.

Think of it as a quality certification — like ISO for manufacturing, but designed specifically for healthcare. When a hospital gets NABH accredited, it means an independent team of experts has verified that the hospital meets specific standards across every department — from how patients are admitted, to how medicines are stored, to how staff are trained, and how infections are prevented.

📊 Did you know? As of today, over 29,000 healthcare facilities across India are either NABH accredited or in the process of getting there. That number is growing every year.

Why Should a Hospital Pursue NABH Accreditation?

The accreditation process is demanding. It takes time, effort, and commitment from the entire team. So is it really worth it? Here is the honest answer — yes. Here is why.

  • 1
    It Forces You to Build Systems That Protect Patients

    The biggest benefit of NABH accreditation is not the certificate on the wall. It is what happens inside the hospital while you are preparing for it. You build proper medication management protocols. You establish infection control committees. You document patient safety incidents and actually learn from them. These systems save lives — not someday, but every single day.
  • 2
    Patients Are Becoming More Informed

    Today's patients — especially in urban and semi-urban areas — Google the hospital before they visit. They ask whether it is NABH accredited. Corporate employees whose companies have health insurance policies are specifically directed to NABH accredited facilities. NABH accreditation is increasingly becoming a patient's first filter, not an afterthought.
  • 3
    Insurance and Government Empanelment Require It

    If your hospital wants to be empanelled under government health schemes — PMJAY, CGHS, state health missions — NABH accreditation gives you a significant advantage. Many schemes either prefer or mandate it. Private health insurance companies also factor in accreditation when deciding empanelment and reimbursement rates. An accredited hospital typically negotiates better terms.
  • 4
    It Reduces Medical Errors and Liability

    Hospitals without proper systems make more errors — wrong medication dosages, missed follow-ups, inadequate informed consent. These are not just patient safety issues — they are legal and financial liabilities. NABH accreditation builds the documentation and process discipline that reduces these errors systematically.
  • 5
    Staff Performance and Retention Improves

    When a hospital works toward NABH accreditation, staff understand their roles more clearly. Training improves. Accountability increases. Nurses, technicians, and administrators who care about quality — the good ones — prefer working in an accredited environment.

The Common Misconception: "NABH is Only for Big Hospitals"

This is the single biggest myth I encounter in my work. NABH has programmes specifically designed for small hospitals and clinics.

The SHCO programme — Small Healthcare Organisation — is built for hospitals with up to 50 beds. The Entry Level Certification is a stepping stone designed to make accreditation accessible to hospitals just beginning their quality journey.

Whether you run a 10-bed nursing home or a 200-bed multispecialty hospital, there is an NABH pathway designed for your size and stage.

What Does the Accreditation Process Actually Involve?

The hospital first conducts a self-assessment against NABH standards — identifying gaps across all departments. Then it implements improvements, trains staff, builds documentation, and runs internal audits to verify readiness. Finally, NABH assessors visit and conduct the formal assessment.

The most common challenge hospitals face is not the assessment itself — it is the preparation. Tracking hundreds of standards, managing audit schedules, maintaining documentation, and keeping the entire team aligned requires significant effort, especially if you are doing it manually.

Where Should You Start?

If your hospital has not yet started the NABH journey, the first step is an honest internal assessment. Walk through your own hospital with fresh eyes. Ask yourself — if an NABH assessor walked in today, what would they find?

Most hospitals find gaps they were not aware of. That is not a failure. That is the beginning of improvement.

Start with the basics: patient safety goals, medication management, infection control, and documentation. Build your committees. Train your staff. Create a culture where quality is everyone's responsibility, not just the quality manager's.

NABH accreditation is not a destination. It is a discipline. The hospitals that understand this are the ones that genuinely improve — and the ones that their patients trust most.
