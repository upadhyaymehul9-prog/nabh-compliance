Why generic SOP templates don't work for NABH

A search for "hospital SOP template" turns up thousands of generic documents — written for no particular standards book, no particular edition, and no particular accreditation body. They're a starting point for format, not content. An assessor isn't checking whether you have an SOP; they're checking whether it matches the specific objective element it's meant to satisfy, and whether your Records prove it's actually followed.

What makes AccredReady's SOPs different

  • Mapped to the standard, not generic. Each SOP is tied to the specific NABH objective element it supports, inside your AccredReady dashboard.
  • Paired with the matching Policy and Records. An SOP without its governing Policy and the Records that evidence it is incomplete — all three come as a set.
  • Editable from the start. Downloads as a Word document, not a locked PDF, so you can adapt the specific steps to your hospital's actual layout, staffing, and equipment before circulating it.
  • Personalised automatically. Your hospital's name is filled in on download — no manual find-and-replace across dozens of documents.

How to use an SOP template properly

Download it, then treat it as a first draft, not a final one. Walk it against how your department actually works — if the SOP describes a step your hospital doesn't have the equipment or staffing for, change it before you circulate it for sign-off. An SOP that describes a process you can't actually follow is a liability at survey time, not an asset.
