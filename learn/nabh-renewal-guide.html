NABH reaccreditation and renewal — what changes the second time

For hospitals that already hold HCO Full, SHCO Full, or Entry Level Certification — how to renew without a gap, and why the second assessment is not a photocopy of the first.

MU Dr. Mehul Upadhyay · Healthcare Operations Leader · Published: August 2026

NABH HCO Full Accreditation is valid for 4 years under the Hospital Accreditation Standards 6th Edition (effective 1 January 2025). Entry Level Certification is a 2-year certification cycle on the Entry-Level Certification Programme (2nd Edition) page. For HCO Full, NABH states the accredited hospital has to apply for Renewal Assessment 6 months prior to expiry, with Surveillance Assessment at 21–24 months. SHCO Full validity should be confirmed on your certificate and current nabh.co SHCO documents (NABH FAQs describe most accreditations as three to four years with mid-cycle surveillance). Renewal is not a lighter first assessment — assessors expect sustained practice and closed-loop CAPA. Source: nabh.co / HCO 6th Edition.

The renewal trap: hospitals celebrate accreditation, then freeze the quality system until year 3 (or month 18 for ELC). The second assessor team notices immediately — stale committee minutes, KPI registers without analysis, and CAPAs that never closed.

What changes the second time

First-cycle assessments often reward getting the system stood up. Reaccreditation rewards proving the system ran continuously.

First assessment

  • → Can you show SOPs exist and staff know them?
  • → Do you have ~3 months of KPI data?
  • → Are mandatory committees formed with minutes?
  • → Are open CAPAs closed before the visit?

Reaccreditation

  • → Did practice stay live across years, not months?
  • → Do KPIs show trends, outliers, and action?
  • → Did committees meet with quorum across the cycle?
  • → Did CAPA themes recur — or get fixed?

Under NABH HCO 6th Edition (2024), final-assessment scoring rules tighten at later cycle points — including stricter treatment of low-scoring objective elements at re-accreditation. Do not assume a 5th Edition playbook still maps. Run a current-edition gap analysis. Source: NABH HCO 6th Edition guidebook, nabh.co.

Renewal timeline — Full Accreditation (4-year cycle)

  1. 1

    Month −18 to −15 — baseline again

    Re-score every chapter against the current edition. Flag CORE gaps, statutory FMS items (fire NOC, BMW, AERB), and services added since first accreditation.

  2. 2

    Month −15 to −9 — close drift

    Refresh SOPs that no longer match practice. Rebuild training records for staff who joined after the first award. Restart weak committee rhythms.

  3. 3

    Month −9 to −4 — internal audits + mock assessment

    Complete at least two honest internal audit cycles with CAPA closure. Run a mock assessment using the assessment preparation guide.

  4. 4

    Month −4 to expiry — portal, desktop, on-site

    Submit on the NABH portal with current evidence. Address desktop observations before the on-site visit. Keep the Medical Director and department heads calendar-blocked for assessment days.

Renewal timeline — Entry Level (2-year cycle)

ELC renewal is shorter and more unforgiving of delay because the certificate window is only 2 years and the PMJAY 10% incentive usually depends on continuous validity.

  • →Month −9: Gap analysis against current ELC standards; confirm committee and KPI continuity.
  • →Month −6: Close CAPAs; refresh training; verify statutory documents under FMS.
  • →Month −3: Submit renewal on the NABH portal; prepare SHA certificate re-upload for the incentive tier.
  • →After award: Re-submit the new certificate to the SHA so the enhanced rate does not drop. See state SHA activation steps.

Evidence assessors expect at renewal

Area First cycle often accepts Renewal expects
KPIs 3 months of registers Longer trend, analysis, action on poor indicators
Committees Formation + early minutes Quorum, action taken, recurring agenda across years
Internal audit 2 cycles before assessment Ongoing calendar with closed CAPA themes
Staff interviews Trained for assessment week New joiners oriented continuously
FMS / statutory Documents current at visit No lapses across the cycle; renewals tracked

Cost of a renewal gap

A lapsed certificate is not “a few weeks of paperwork.” Marketing must stop claiming current NABH status. Empanelment partners may ask for a valid certificate. For PMJAY-empanelled hospitals, the SHA tier typically falls back to base rates until a new certificate is filed — which can erase months of state incentive value. Fee details for re-application live on the NABH cost page; verify live figures at nabh.co.

Frequently asked questions

How long is NABH Full Accreditation valid?

HCO Full is 4 years under HCO 6th Edition (effective 1 January 2025). Entry Level Certification is a 2-year cycle on the ELC 2nd Edition page. Confirm SHCO Full validity on your certificate and current nabh.co SHCO documents.

When should a hospital start NABH renewal preparation?

For HCO Full, NABH requires the renewal application 6 months prior to expiry — start internal prep earlier. ELC: begin 6–9 months before expiry and follow the ELC renewal matrix on nabh.co.

Is NABH reaccreditation easier than the first assessment?

Familiarity helps; expectations often rise. Assessors look for sustained implementation and multi-year quality behaviour. Under HCO 6th Edition, certain re-accreditation scoring rules are stricter than the first final assessment. Source: nabh.co.

What happens if NABH accreditation expires before renewal?

Accredited status lapses until a new certificate is issued. PMJAY tiers typically revert to base rate. Stop public claims of current NABH status until you are certified again.

Do I need a fresh gap analysis for NABH renewal?

Yes. Re-score against the current edition and your current service mix. Do not recycle the first-cycle spreadsheet unchanged.

Sources: Programme validity and assessment framework from NABH programme documentation at nabh.co and the NABH HCO 6th Edition (2024) guidebook. Preparation lead times are practitioner estimates for hospital quality teams.
