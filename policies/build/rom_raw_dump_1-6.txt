STANDARD: ROM.1 — Those Responsible for Governance
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Standard title (verbatim): "The organisation identifies those responsible for
  governance and their roles are defined."

OE: ROM.1.a | Level: CORE ★
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"Those responsible for governance are identified and their roles and
responsibilities are defined and documented."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
Those responsible for governance are accountable for the quality of care and
help the healthcare organisation to achieve its goals. The structure of the
hospital's governing body/entity is described in a written document. The
document(s) describe when and how the authority of the governing entity can be
delegated and to whom. The terms of reference, by-laws and membership of those
responsible for governance is documented. This may require that those
responsible for governance receive formal orientation regarding their role.
Those responsible for governance meet at defined intervals and minutes of the
meeting are maintained.

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

MANDATORY (shall):
- Governing body/entity structure described in a written document
- Delegation of authority — when and how and to whom — documented
- Terms of reference, by-laws and membership documented
- Meet at defined intervals; minutes of meetings maintained

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- Formal orientation for governing body members on their role

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Named list of those responsible, with roles and
responsibilities, plus governance minutes confirming identification, required.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. Named list of those responsible for governance, with each member's role
   and responsibility documented.
2. Governance resolution or minute formally identifying the list.
3. Dated record of the last review or update of the list.

OE: ROM.1.b | Level: Commitment ★
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"Those responsible for governance lay down the organisation's vision, mission
and values."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
The organisation shall enunciate its vision, mission and values through an
authorised document. These shall be developed and reviewed by those responsible
for governance in consultation with the organisation's leaders. Further, inputs
could be from external stakeholders, including patients, families and community.
The vision, mission and values of the organisation shall be displayed
prominently. Only a display on its website would not be appropriate. The same
could be translated and displayed in the local language also. For the definition
of "mission", "vision" and "values" refer to the glossary.

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

MANDATORY (shall):
- Vision, mission and values enacted through an authorised (governance-approved)
  document
- Developed/reviewed by governance in consultation with leaders
- Displayed prominently (website alone is not sufficient)

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- External stakeholder inputs (patients, families, community)
- Translation and display in local language

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Governance-approved VM&V document with approval
date, plus evidence of public display, required.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. Governance-approved vision, mission and values statement, with approval date.
2. Evidence of public display — photograph or location log, website page, or
   induction-pack copy.
3. Record of when the statement was last reviewed or reaffirmed.

VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"Those responsible for governance approve the documented strategic and
operational plans and the organisation's annual budget."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
The methodology and frequency of preparation of strategic plan may differ
according to the size and type of organisation. Documented operational plans and
budget / expenditure shall be approved annually. The operational plan shall be
linked to the strategic plan. The strategic and operational plans shall identify
responsibility and possible timeframes for achievement. The documented annual
budget shall include both capital expenditure and operating expenditure. Refer to
the glossary for "strategic and operational plans".

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

MANDATORY (shall):
- Operational plans and budget approved annually
- Operational plan linked to the strategic plan
- Plans identify responsibility and timeframes
- Annual budget includes both capital and operating expenditure

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- Frequency of strategic plan preparation may vary by organisation size/type

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Governance minutes approving the plans and budget
before the cycle starts, plus the approved documents themselves, required.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. Governance minutes approving the strategic plan, operational plan and annual
   budget before the cycle starts.
2. The approved strategic plan, operational plan and budget documents themselves.
3. Dated resolution or sign-off evidencing approval, not just circulation.

OE: ROM.2.a | Level: CORE ★
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"The leaders establish the organisation's ethical management framework."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
The organisation shall function ethically. Transparency in its actions shall be
one of its guiding principles. Handling of complaints, grievances, clinical care
delivery and research shall be some of the areas to address. The framework
includes codes of conduct. A good reference guide for minimum code of conduct for
doctors is "Code of medical ethics, 2002" published by Medical Council of India.

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

MANDATORY (shall):
- Organisation functions ethically; transparency is a guiding principle
- Framework addresses: complaints, grievances, clinical care delivery, research
- Framework includes codes of conduct

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- MCI Code of Medical Ethics 2002 as a reference (not a compliance mandate for
  this OE)

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Written ethical-management framework document with
approval and ownership, required. Minutes-only notes are not sufficient.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. The written ethical-management framework: principles, decision authority and
   escalation path.
2. Dated approval record naming who owns the framework.
3. Evidence the framework is a standing document, not minutes-only notes.

OE: ROM.2.b | Level: Commitment ★
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"The ethical management framework includes processes for managing issues with
ethical implications, dilemmas and concerns."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
The issues may be clinical or non-clinical. Some examples include conflict of
interest, breach of confidentiality, ethical behaviour, informed consent, and
withdrawing life-sustaining treatment. There shall be a consistent approach to
ethical decision making throughout the organisation based on the guiding
principle(s). Staff shall be provided with appropriate guidance with respect to
their actions based on these principles. The approach shall include process for
collecting, collating and analysing ethical dilemmas for taking corrective and
preventive actions within a defined time frame. Ethical dilemma could arise when
there are conflicting decisions regarding provision or withdrawal of life support
or treatment. Decisions made regarding ethical issues shall be documented, and
opportunities for improvement could be identified.

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

MANDATORY (shall):
- Consistent approach to ethical decision making throughout the organisation
- Staff provided with guidance based on the ethical principles
- Process for collecting, collating and analysing ethical dilemmas
- CAPA taken within a defined time frame
- Decisions regarding ethical issues documented

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- Conflict of interest, breach of confidentiality, informed consent, withdrawing
  life-sustaining treatment (examples of issue types; hospital names its own list)
- Opportunities for improvement identification

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Named escalation path and timeline, plus log of
ethical issues raised with resolution and date, required.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. Named escalation path and timeline for handling an ethical issue, dilemma or
   concern.
2. Log of ethical issues raised, with resolution and date.
3. At least one example case record showing the process was actually used.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"Those responsible for governance address the organisation's sustainability
programme in terms of Environment Social and Governance (ESG) responsibility."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
Environmental sustainability includes energy usage and efficiency, climate change
strategy, waste reduction, biodiversity loss, greenhouse gas emissions and carbon
footprint reduction. Social sustainability includes fair pay and living wages,
equal employment opportunity, employee benefits, workplace health and safety,
community engagement, responsible supply chain partnerships, adhering to labour
laws. Governance sustainability includes corporate governance, risk management,
compliance, ethical business practises, avoiding conflicts of interest, accounting
integrity and transparency.

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

MANDATORY (shall):
- Sustainability programme addressed by governance in ESG terms

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- Environmental: energy usage/efficiency, climate change strategy, waste
  reduction, greenhouse gas emissions, carbon footprint reduction
- Social: fair pay, equal employment, employee benefits, workplace health and
  safety, community engagement, responsible supply chain, labour laws
- Governance: corporate governance, risk management, compliance, ethical business
  practices, conflicts of interest, accounting integrity and transparency

DOCUMENTATION NOTE: No star.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. Written ESG sustainability programme with a named owner.
2. Governance-meeting agenda or minutes tabling the programme.
3. Annual review record of the programme.

VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"The organisation takes initiatives towards an energy-efficient and
environmentally friendly hospital."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
This includes using the concepts of reduce, recycle and reuse in promoting the
basic concepts of the green hospital. Energy-efficient lighting, rainwater
harvesting, increase usage of solar power, wind energy, use of battery-operated

examples. The organisation shall focus on efficient and sustainable use of energy,
water and other utilities. The organisation shall take measures to create
awareness among staff and patients regarding saving electricity and water.

STOP-WORK CHECK: No stop-work trigger on this OE.

  electricity and water

  STP/ETP water recycling, plastic reduction, green construction materials, VOC-
  free paints (illustrative list; hospital defines what applies to its context)
- Reduce, recycle, reuse framing

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Dated log of energy-efficiency and environmental

REQUIRED RECORDS:

OE: ROM.6.a | Level: CORE ★
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VERBATIM OE TEXT:
"Leadership ensures proactive risk management across the organisation."

GUIDEBOOK INTERPRETATION:
Risk management shall include clinical and non-clinical (strategic, financial,
operational and hazard) risks. It shall include risk identification at every level
of the organisation, analysis, prioritisation and risk alleviation. The same shall
be documented. At a minimum, analysis of potential risks must include the
likelihood of its occurrence and the potential severity of the impact or
consequences. The identified risks shall be documented in a risk register, which
shall be updated at regular intervals. This shall be documented as a "risk
management plan". It shall include the various risks identified, the action taken
for risk alleviation of each of these risks and the mechanism for informing staff
regarding the same. Other components of the risk management plan include
contingency plans and education and training of staff. Further, the risk
management plan shall be monitored and reviewed for continued effectiveness at
least annually. The results of the review shall be communicated to the relevant
stakeholders in the organisation. This could be done using a matrix.
Clinical-risk assessment could include:
* Medication management (patient/service-user allergies, antimicrobial resistance,
  cytotoxic drugs, narcotics and controlled drugs management)
* Equipment risks — fire/injury from LASER, cautery
* Use of ionising radiation, radioactive isotopes and nuclear medicine
* Risks from long-term conditions
* Patient falls
* Infections
* Risks related to vulnerable patients (e.g. Deep Venous Thrombosis)
* Risk associated with clinical alarms

STOP-WORK CHECK: No direct stop-work trigger on this OE. The proactive risk
management system is what identifies the conditions that make other stop-work
triggers fire (ROM.6.d, ROM.6.e).

MANDATORY (shall):
- Risk management includes clinical and non-clinical risks (strategic, financial,
  operational and hazard)
- Risk identification at every level of the organisation
- Analysis includes at minimum: likelihood of occurrence and severity of
  impact/consequences
- Identified risks documented in a risk register, updated at regular intervals
- Risk management plan documented, including: identified risks, alleviation
  actions, mechanism for informing staff, contingency plans, education and
  training
- Risk management plan monitored and reviewed for effectiveness at least annually
- Results of review communicated to relevant stakeholders

ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY:
- Clinical risk categories listed above (examples; hospital defines its own
  complete risk register)
- Matrix format for risk assessment

DOCUMENTATION NOTE (starred): Risk management system document, risk register,
and assessment/action/review log required.

REQUIRED RECORDS:
1. Risk-management system document.
2. Risk register showing identified clinical and organisational risks (for
   example falls, infection, vulnerable-patient risks such as DVT, clinical
   alarms).
3. Assessment, action and review log for a sample of risks on the register.
