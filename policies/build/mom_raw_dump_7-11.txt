CHAPTER INTENT (official Standards PDF, verbatim):
The organisation has a safe and organised medication management process. Availability,
safe storage, prescription, dispensing and administration of medications are governed by
written guidance. A medication safety officer is designated. The hospital formulary is
developed, implemented and updated. Pharmacy has oversight of medications stocked out of
the pharmacy. Reconciliation occurs at transition points. Patients are monitored after
administration. Near misses, medication errors and adverse drug reactions are reported and
analysed. Medications also include blood, implants and devices. Medical supplies and
consumables are available for use.

MOM.7.a
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Medications are administered by those who are permitted by law to do so.

Guidebook interpretation:
Only a registered nurse or doctor with a minimum of MBBS qualification shall administer
medication. In case there is any other category of staff authorised to administer
medication, a legislation or government order shall back the same.

Stop-work check: Commitment. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: A person who is not on the
organisation's authorised list (or who does not meet the statutory minimum qualification)
must not administer medication. This OE is explicitly named in the MOM.7 stop-work
section ("person not permitted by law"). Immediate life-saving administration in a crash
continues with the best available permitted staff and is documented afterward.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Only a registered nurse or doctor (minimum MBBS) shall administer medication —
     this is the statutory floor stated in the Guidebook.
  2. Any other category of staff authorised to administer medication shall be backed by
     a legislation or government order — the backing must be documentary, not informal.

Illustrative only:
  — No "for example" or "preferable" clauses in this OE's interpretation.
    Both elements above carry "shall".

Statute note: This OE names applicable law. The policy's Statute P2 disclaimer covers it.
Do not reproduce in the policy draft as a legal opinion or specific statutory reference —
state that staff must comply with applicable professional and drugs legislation.

Documentation: No star — the authorised personnel list (held by Nursing Superintendent
and Medical Superintendent) and any supervised-student record are the principal records,
not a separate starred artifact. The list itself is an explicit practical requirement.

MOM.7.b
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Prepared medication is labelled before preparation of a second drug.

Guidebook interpretation:
Labelling is required when more than one drug is prepared and loaded. Examples of these
are anaesthetic drug preparation in OTs, chemotherapy drugs etc.

Stop-work check: Commitment. Named in the MOM.7 stop-work section as a variant of the
"verifying medication against the order" cluster. Two unlabelled syringes on the same tray
is a stop-work condition anywhere in the organisation (OT, ICU, emergency, wards).

Mandatory (shall):
  1. A prepared medication shall be labelled before a second drug is prepared and loaded —
     the sequence is mandatory: label first drug, then prepare next.

Illustrative only:
  — OTs (anaesthetic drugs) and chemotherapy drugs are examples. The requirement is
    general: any location where more than one drug is prepared and loaded simultaneously.

Documentation: No star — the process is the requirement. Evidence is the observable
absence of unlabelled syringes on observation rounds and training records.

MOM.7.c
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The patient is identified before administration.

Guidebook interpretation:
At a minimum, two identifiers shall be used for patient identification, with one of them
being the unique identification number (for example hospital number/IP number, etc.) and
full name of the patient. Refer to COP.1.b.

[Guidebook cross-ref: COP.1.b — in policy draft: "those requirements are covered in the
hospital's other policies."]

Stop-work check: Commitment. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: Administration without completing
two-identifier patient identification is explicitly named in the MOM.7 stop-work section.
If identity cannot be confirmed, administration stops.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. At least two identifiers shall be used — the minimum is two; the organisation may
     require more.
  2. One identifier shall be the unique identification number (hospital number / IP number
     or equivalent).
  3. One identifier shall be the full name of the patient.

Illustrative only:
  — Hospital number and IP number are examples of acceptable unique identification numbers;
    other unique identifiers that meet the requirement are permissible.
  — Asking an attendant or matching by bed number alone does not satisfy the requirement
    (this is an implicit exclusion from the two-identifier standard, confirmed by the
    method_bodies commentary).

Documentation: No star — identification is evidenced by the administration record entry
and by observation. No separate starred artifact.

MOM.7.d
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Medication is verified from the medication order and physically inspected before
administration.

Guidebook interpretation:
Staff administering medications shall verify the medication order and ensure that
medications are administered appropriately. The general appearance of the medication
(for example melting, clumping, etc.) and the expiry dates shall be checked before
administration. If any of the parameters concerning an order, such as name, strength,
route or frequency or time are missing/incomplete, the medication administration shall be
deferred, pending early verification by the treating team. In case the confirmation is
obtained verbally, it shall be considered a verbal order and the procedure for verbal
orders shall be adhered to. In case of high-risk medication(s), the verification shall be
done by at least two staff (nurse-nurse or nurse-doctor), independently and documented.
The nurses shall be knowledgeable regarding high-risk medications and shall be empowered
to highlight prescription errors noted while verifying the orders.

Stop-work check: CORE. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: The MOM.7 stop-work section fires when
administration proceeds without verifying medication/strength/route/timing against the
order, or when the product fails physical inspection. A missing or incomplete parameter
(name, strength, route, frequency, time) requires deferral, not a workaround.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Staff shall verify the medication order before administration.
  2. General appearance (melting, clumping, etc.) and expiry date shall be checked before
     administration.
  3. If any parameter (name, strength, route, frequency or time) is missing or incomplete,
     administration shall be deferred pending early verification by the treating team.
  4. If verbal confirmation is obtained, it shall be treated as a verbal order and the
     MOM.4.f verbal-order process (read-back, countersignature within 24 hours) shall apply.
  5. For high-risk medications, verification shall be done by at least two staff
     (nurse-nurse or nurse-doctor), independently and documented.
  6. Nurses shall be knowledgeable about high-risk medications and empowered to highlight
     prescription errors during verification.

Illustrative only:
  — "Melting, clumping" are examples of general-appearance defects; the check is
    for any physical abnormality, not only those two.

CORE status: Administering without verifying the order or without physical inspection is
a CORE gap and a stop-work trigger.

Documentation: No star — evidence is the administration record, the verification
annotation for high-risk medication checks, and training records.

MOM.7.g
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Timing is verified from the order before administration.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation shall have documentation to support the time of administration of drugs
for which the time has not been written. For example, 1-1-1, BD etc. The suggested
timings for these medicines have to be adhered to. "ISMP Acute Care Guidelines for Timely

Stop-work check: Commitment. Named in the MOM.7 stop-work section ("without verifying

organisation's allowed window without prescriber review (except documented emergency use)
is a stop-work condition.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. The organisation shall have documentation to support the time of administration for
     drugs where the time has not been explicitly written on the order (e.g., standard
     frequency codes such as 1-1-1, BD).
  2. Suggested timings for such medicines shall be adhered to.

Illustrative only:
  — ISMP Acute Care Guidelines: the organisation "could adopt / adapt" — permissive;
    these are a good reference but adoption is not mandatory.
  — 1-1-1 and BD are examples of frequency codes that require a timing lookup;
    not an exhaustive list of the types that need it.

Documentation: No star — evidenced by the timing-documentation record (e.g., pharmacy
or nursing-defined drug timing schedule) and the administration chart entries showing

MOM.7.h
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Measures to avoid catheter and tubing mis-connections during medication administration
are implemented.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation ensures that inadvertent administration of a drug through the wrong
route is avoided. This could be done by using design features that prevent misconnections
and prompt the user to take the correct action. Intravenous (IV) extension tubes shall
not be used for epidurals, irrigation, drains, and central lines or to extend enteric
feeding tubes. Care shall be taken to position functionally dissimilar tubes used in
patient care away from one another. Staff administering medications shall trace all lines
from their origin to the connection port to verify attachments before making any
connections or reconnections, or administering medications, solutions, or other products.

[Note: The Guidebook JSON for MOM.7.h ends with "Medication administration is
documented." — this is an OCR-transcription artefact from MOM.7.i bleeding into the
MOM.7.h block. That sentence belongs to MOM.7.i and is not an additional requirement
under MOM.7.h.]

Stop-work check: CORE and starred. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: The MOM.7 stop-work section
explicitly names "tubing mis-connection" as a stop-work condition. Connecting a catheter
or tubing for medication administration without tracing the line from the patient to the
source is the trigger. Untreated mis-connection risk must be resolved before
administration proceeds.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. The organisation shall ensure that inadvertent wrong-route administration is avoided —
     this is an outcome-level mandatory obligation; the organisation chooses its means.
  2. IV extension tubes shall not be used for epidurals, irrigation, drains, central lines,
     or to extend enteric feeding tubes — explicit prohibition; no exceptions stated.
  3. Care shall be taken to position functionally dissimilar tubes away from one another.
  4. Staff shall trace all lines from their origin to the connection port before making any
     connections, reconnections, or before administering medications/solutions/products —
     explicit line-tracing requirement; mandatory, not advisory.

Illustrative only:
  — Design features preventing misconnections "could be done" — this is one means of
    compliance, not the only means. The obligation is the outcome (wrong-route avoided),
    not a specific engineering solution. ISO NRFit / colour-coded connectors are good
    practice but are not stated as mandatory here.

CORE status: Failure to implement measures against tubing mis-connection is a CORE gap
and a stop-work trigger.

Documentation: OE is starred — written measures (SOP, training records for ICU/OT/
emergency/ward staff on line tracing) must be on file and demonstrably in use.

MOM.7.i
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Medication administration is documented.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation shall ensure that documentation of medication administration is done in
a uniform location. It shall include the name of the medication, strength, route of
administration, timing and the name/employee ID number and signature of the person who
has administered the medication. Medicines administered shall be documented each time for
each dose of the same medication separately. The records shall reflect the actual
administration. For example, if brand Y was given in place of brand X (same generically),
the documentation shall be of brand Y. Similarly, if the order was for a tablet of 250 mg
but the administration was ½ a tablet of 500 mg, the latter shall be documented. In the
case of infusions, it shall capture the start time, the rate / volume of infusion and end
time. In case of continuous infusion, the drop rate / volume shall be documented and the
total volume infused shall be calculated for each shift.

[Portal PDF note: The official PDF prints this objective element as "I." (uppercase i
or Roman numeral I) rather than "i" in some printings. The policy treats it as MOM.7.i
consistently, as the inventory JSON confirms.]

Stop-work check: Commitment — documentation failure is not itself a stop-work trigger in
the MOM.7 stop-work section, which fires on administration safety conditions. However,
absent or shift-end-batch documentation is an audit finding.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Documentation shall be in a uniform location.
  2. Documentation shall include: medication name, strength, route of administration,
     timing, and the name / employee ID / signature of the administrator.
  3. Each dose shall be documented separately — one documentation entry per dose of
     each medication; batch charting at shift-end is not compliant.
  4. Documentation shall reflect the actual administration, not the order as written.
     (If brand Y was administered in place of brand X of the same generic, document Y.
     If ½ × 500 mg was given instead of 250 mg tablet, document the actual form given.)
  5. For infusions: start time, rate / volume of infusion, and end time shall be captured.
  6. For continuous infusions: drop rate / volume shall be documented, and total volume
     infused shall be calculated for each shift.

Illustrative only:
  — Brand Y / brand X substitution and ½-tablet examples are illustrative of the
    "actual administration" documentation principle; not the only scenarios where
    actual-vs-ordered divergence must be documented.

Documentation: No star — the medication chart is itself the record; a separate starred
artifact is not required by this OE. However, the chart must comply with the six
elements listed above.

MOM.7.j
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Measures to govern patient's self-administration of medications are implemented.

Guidebook interpretation:
At the outset, the organisation could define if it would permit the self-administration
of medications. In case the organisation permits the same, the written guidance shall
define the medications which the patient can self-administer. It is preferable that the
organisation also incorporates a method for assisting self-administration of medications.
The organisation shall ensure that the patient is reminded to take the medication
(before every dose) and document the same. For example, self-administration of insulin.

Stop-work check: Starred Commitment — not a direct stop-work trigger. However, uncontrolled
bedside hoarding of hospital stock (informal self-administration without written measures)
is an implied serious finding against this OE.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. A decision shall be made (and documented) on whether the organisation permits
     patient self-administration — the Guidebook's "could define" signals that the
     choice is the organisation's, but a written decision either way is required because
     the OE mandates "measures to govern."
  2. If self-administration is permitted, written guidance shall define which medications
     the patient may self-administer.
  3. If self-administration is permitted, the organisation shall ensure the patient is
     reminded to take the medication before every dose.
  4. Reminders shall be documented.

Illustrative only:
  — Assisting self-administration is "preferable" — aspiration; not a mandatory
    programme element.
  — Insulin is an example of a self-administered medication; not the only permitted type.

Documentation: OE is starred — the written self-administration measures (or the written
decision that self-administration is not permitted and staff instructions to that effect)
must be on file. Reminder and dose-documentation records are also explicit.

MOM.8.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Patients shall be monitored after medication administration.

Guidebook interpretation:
Relevant monitoring shall be done collaboratively to verify that the medicine is having
its intended effect. It could also include monitoring the effects of medications
(beneficial or adverse) through laboratory results. Besides, this shall help identify
near misses, medication errors and adverse drug reactions. The organisation shall define
those situations and medications where more frequent monitoring is required. For example,
administration of high-risk medicines. The effect of medication in high risk patients like
those on dialysis, in the ICU and elderly group shall be monitored on a regular basis.

Stop-work check: Starred Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger. MOM.8 has no
stop-work authority. Monitoring failure is an audit finding and, if it leads to harm, an
incident that triggers MOM.8.c–f capture and analysis.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Relevant monitoring shall be done collaboratively to verify the medicine is having
     its intended effect.
  2. The organisation shall define situations and medications where more frequent
     monitoring is required.
  3. The effect of medication in high-risk patients (those on dialysis, in the ICU,
     and elderly group) shall be monitored on a regular basis — these specific categories
     are stated with "shall" and represent the floor, not a ceiling.

Illustrative only:
  — Monitoring through laboratory results is listed as something monitoring "could also
    include" — permissive; labs are one method, not the only required method.
  — High-risk medicines are given as an example of situations requiring more frequent
    monitoring; the organisation's own definition of which situations qualify is the
    mandatory act.
  — Dialysis, ICU and elderly are named as high-risk patient groups requiring regular
    monitoring; the Guidebook's "like" signals these are examples, not an exhaustive list.

Documentation: OE is starred — written guidance naming what to monitor and when to
escalate, and recorded monitoring evidence for sampled cases, must be on file.
