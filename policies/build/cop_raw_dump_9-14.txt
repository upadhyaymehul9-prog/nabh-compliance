COP.9.a

Verbatim OE text:

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 120):

Stop-work check: YES — COP.9 is in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. The written guidance

address the other OEs of this standard is a systemic compliance failure.

    this standard (shall — the minimum scope is the full set of OEs below).

Non-mandatory (illustrative/optional):

    the form of clinical bundling is the organisation's decision.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the written guidance is the required
documentation.

DRAFTING NOTE: The "at a minimum, this shall address all the objective elements
of this standard" clause means the written guidance for COP.9 must, on its face,
cover the substance of OEs b through g. This is not common to every standard —
it is explicitly stated here.

COP.9.b
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The defined admission and discharge criteria for intensive care and high
dependency units are implemented.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 120):
The organisation shall develop criteria for admission/transfer-in/discharge/
transfer-out of patients to ICU/HDU based on physiologic parameters and/or
diagnostic parameters, and adhere to the same. The organisation shall have a
mechanism for "discharge against medical advice". A good starting point could
be various national and international critical care society guidelines.

Staff are trained to apply these criteria.

Stop-work check: YES — COP.9 stop-work. Admitting or discharging an ICU/HDU
patient without applying defined criteria — or the absence of defined criteria —
is a direct patient-safety trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  — Criteria for admission/transfer-in/discharge/transfer-out shall be developed
    (shall) based on physiologic parameters and/or diagnostic parameters (shall).
  — The organisation shall adhere to the defined criteria (shall — not aspirational;
    "implemented" in the OE text means compliance is required).
  — A mechanism for discharge against medical advice shall exist (shall).
  — Staff shall be trained to apply these criteria (shall — "staff are trained"
    is a stated requirement, not advisory).

Non-mandatory:
  — National and international critical care society guidelines are described as
    "a good starting point" — advisory reference, not a mandatory source.
  — Whether criteria are physiologic-parameter-based, diagnostic-parameter-based,
    or both is the organisation's clinical decision.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the documented admission/discharge criteria
are the required documentation.

DRAFTING NOTE: The criteria cover four directions: admission, transfer-in,
discharge, and transfer-out. Do not collapse these to "admission and discharge
criteria" — transfer-in and transfer-out are distinct scenarios (e.g., step-down
from ICU to ward, transfer to another ICU).

COP.9.d
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Defined procedures for the situation of bed shortages are followed.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 120):

Stop-work check: YES — COP.9 stop-work. The absence of a defined procedure for

Mandatory (shall):

    situations (shall — "shall be in place" is explicit; the procedure must exist
    before the situation arises, not be improvised when it occurs).
  — The procedures shall be followed (the OE text uses "are followed" — compliance
    is required, not discretionary).

Non-mandatory:
  — The specific content of the bed-shortage procedure (step-down, inter-facility
    transfer, escalation chain) is the organisation's determination.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the policy and procedure document is the
required documentation.

DRAFTING NOTE: The Guidebook trigger is "as and when" — the procedure must be
activated whenever ICU beds are full and a patient requires ICU care. It does not
apply only to predicted or elective demand.

COP.9.g
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation has a mechanism to counsel the patient and/or family
periodically.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 121):
Patients and families are counselled by a doctor of the treating team at periodic
intervals (at least once a day) and when there is a significant change in the
condition of the patient. Significant events since the last counselling session,
expected outcomes, and queries related to the changing condition of the patient
shall be addressed. Counselling shall be documented and/or audio video
recorded. It is preferable to obtain an acknowledgement for the same.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no real-time stop-work trigger from individual
counselling delays; however, systematic absence of any counselling mechanism
is a compliance failure.

EXACT MANDATORY FREQUENCY — preserve verbatim:
  — "at least once a day" — counselling shall occur at least once a day and
    whenever there is a significant change in the patient's condition.
    Do not soften to "regularly" or "periodically" — the Guidebook specifies
    the minimum.

Mandatory (shall):
  — Counselling shall be conducted by a doctor of the treating team (shall —
    not by nursing staff or other personnel acting as the sole counsellor).
  — Frequency: at least once a day AND on significant change in patient condition
    (shall — both triggers are mandatory).
  — Significant events, expected outcomes, and patient queries related to changing
    condition shall be addressed (shall — three content obligations).
  — Counselling shall be documented and/or audio-video recorded (shall — at least
    one of the two modalities is required).

Non-mandatory:
  — Obtaining an acknowledgement from the family is "preferable" — advisory
    best practice, not a shall.

DRAFTING NOTES — COP.9
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. ONE EXACT FREQUENCY:
   — COP.9.g: "at least once a day" — do not soften to "periodically" or
     "at regular intervals."

2. SCOPE OF WRITTEN GUIDANCE (COP.9.a): The mandatory minimum scope of the
   written guidance is "all objective elements of this standard." This is
   explicitly stated — the policy must address COP.9.a through COP.9.g on
   its face. This is unusual language; flag it in the draft header.

3. FOUR-DIRECTION CRITERIA (COP.9.b): admission, transfer-in, discharge,
   transfer-out — all four directions are stated. Do not collapse.

4. DISCHARGE AGAINST MEDICAL ADVICE (COP.9.b): A mechanism for DAMA must
   exist as a separate stated requirement — it is not subsumed by "discharge
   criteria."

5. COUNSELLING DOCUMENTATION (COP.9.g): Either documentation in writing OR
   audio-video recording satisfies the shall — but at least one modality is
   mandatory. Both simultaneously is not required.

6. STOP-WORK (COP.9): Primary triggers: absent or unapplied admission/discharge
   criteria (COP.9.b), absent bed-shortage procedure (COP.9.d), inadequate ICU
   equipment or uncompetent staff (COP.9.c), IPC practices not followed (COP.9.e).

evidenced-based clinical practice guidelines.

training for obstetric emergencies is a stop-work trigger for any high-risk

  1. Assessment of pregnant patients incorporating nutrition, immunisation and
     education
  2. Ante-natal care guidelines
  3. Peri-natal and post-natal care guidelines

Mandatory (shall):
  — Written guidance shall be based on STG/sound clinical practices (shall).
  — Written guidance shall, at a minimum, include the three areas above (shall —

  — Organisation shall define in written guidance whether care for high-risk
    obstetric cases is provided or not (shall — scope declaration is mandatory).
  — Organisation shall define what constitutes a high-risk obstetric case in

  — Staff shall be trained on management of obstetric emergencies based on
    evidence-based CPGs (shall — training is mandatory, not advisory).

Non-mandatory:
  — Care of new-borns is in accordance with COP.11.b — cross-ref only; do not

    to an appropriate organisation (shall — referral is mandatory when in-scope
    care is unavailable; pro-active means before the patient presents in crisis).
  — Organisations providing high-risk care shall have competent workforce and
    facilities to care for such mothers and neonates (shall).

Non-mandatory:
  — The specific competency criteria for the workforce are the organisation's

COP.14.d
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Care is taken to prevent adverse events like the wrong site, wrong patient and
wrong surgery.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 132–133):
Written guidance shall be available for preventing adverse events like the wrong
site, wrong patient, wrong surgery by a suitable mechanism. The organisation
shall be able to demonstrate methods to prevent these events, for example
identification tags, badges, cross-checks, time-outs etc.

Marking the surgical/invasive site involves the patient and is done with an
instantly recognizable and unambiguous mark. The mark shall be consistent
throughout the organisation. The site is marked in all cases involving laterality,
multiple structures (fingers, toes, lesions), or multiple levels (spine). The
surgical/invasive procedure site marking is done by the person or member of
the team who will perform the procedure. This person will do the entire surgical/
invasive procedure and remain with the patient throughout the entire procedure.
The site marking may take place any time before the surgical/invasive procedure
begins, as long as the patient is actively involved in the site marking whenever
possible. The mark shall be visible in the procedural field when prepping and
draping is complete.

The time-out shall be held immediately before the start of the procedure with all
team members present. During the time-out, the team agrees on the correct
patient identity, correct procedure to be done, correct surgical procedure site.

Stop-work check: CORE — the most critical OE in COP.14. Performing or beginning
a surgical procedure without completing the required site marking and time-out
verification is the primary stop-work trigger.

MANDATORY SITE-MARKING TRIGGERS (all three shall apply):
  1. Laterality — where the procedure involves one of a bilateral pair
  2. Multiple structures — fingers, toes, lesions, etc.
  3. Multiple levels — spine

TIME-OUT MANDATORY REQUIREMENTS — preserve all three:
  1. Held immediately before the start of the procedure
  2. All team members present
  3. Agreement on: correct patient identity + correct procedure + correct surgical site

MANDATORY SITE-MARKING RULES:
  — Patient involvement in site marking is mandatory "whenever possible"
    (shall — the qualifier is part of the rule, not an exemption).
  — Site marking is done by the person (or team member) who will perform the
    procedure AND who will remain with the patient throughout (shall — a
    consistent person requirement).
  — Mark shall be instantly recognizable and unambiguous (shall).
  — Mark shall be consistent throughout the organisation (shall).
  — Mark shall be visible in the procedural field when prepping and draping is
    complete (shall — a functional visibility test).

Mandatory (shall):
  — Written guidance for wrong-site/patient/surgery prevention shall be available
    (shall).
  — All site-marking and time-out rules above shall be complied with (shall).

Non-mandatory (illustrative):
  — Identification tags, badges, cross-checks, time-outs are examples of
    prevention methods — the mechanism chosen by the organisation is its
    determination, subject to the mandatory site-marking and time-out rules.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the written guidance and documentation
of the time-out are the primary documentation requirements.

DRAFTING NOTE: The time-out must be held IMMEDIATELY BEFORE the start of
the procedure — not at prep stage or on entering the OT. The exact timing is
mandatory.
