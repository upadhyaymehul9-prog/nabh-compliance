Standard text (verbatim):
Incidents are collected and analysed to ensure continual quality improvement.

OE count: 6 | COREs: a | Stars: a★, b★


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PSQ.7.a
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation implements an incident management system.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 231):
The incident management system includes:
  * identification
  * reporting
  * review
  * action on incidents

This system supports factual reporting and learning and is based on the
principle of just culture.

The organisation shall have a mechanism for reporting the occurrence of
incidents on standardised incident report forms. It is preferable that the
reporting system is simple (a few steps), clear (what needs to be reported,
how to report, and to whom), confidential, and focused on process improvement.

While capturing the incidents, the organisation shall capture all incidents
without going into the severity or whether harm was caused.

Stop-work check: CORE — non-negotiable. Absence of an implemented incident
management system is CORE non-compliance. Stop-work is implicated when any
component of the system (identification, reporting, review, action) is missing
— the four components are all mandatory elements of the system, not optional
enhancements.

Two mandatory (shall) requirements within the interpretation:
  1. A mechanism for reporting on standardised incident report forms shall
     exist.
  2. All incidents shall be captured regardless of severity or whether harm
     was caused — pre-filtering by severity or harm level before capture is
     prohibited.

The good-practice characteristics (simple, clear, confidential, focused on
process improvement) are preferable, not mandatory. Just-culture basis is
stated as a principle, not a shall.

This OE is asterisked — documentation of the incident management system is
required.

PSQ.7.b
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation has a mechanism to identify sentinel events.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 231):
The sentinel events relating to system or process deficiencies that are
relevant and important to the organisation must be clearly defined. The list
of the identified and relevant sentinel events shall be documented.

Refer to the glossary for a definition of "sentinel events".

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

Two mandatory requirements:
  1. The organisation's relevant sentinel events must be clearly defined —
     "must" is used, which carries obligatory force equivalent to shall.
  2. The list of identified and relevant sentinel events shall be documented.

CRITICAL DRAFTING NOTE — THIS IS THE ACTUAL SOURCE FOR SENTINEL-EVENT
DEFINITIONS IN PSQ:

The Guidebook at PSQ.7.b does NOT prescribe any enumerated list of sentinel
event categories. It instructs the organisation to:
  (a) determine which sentinel events are relevant to its own systems and
      processes, and
  (b) clearly define and document that self-identified list.

The definition of "sentinel events" is referred to the Glossary — it is not
reproduced within PSQ.7.b itself.

An earlier ChatGPT draft for PSQ.2 included an enumerated list of sentinel
event categories attributed to PSQ.2 source text. That list had no source in
NABH — PSQ.2 contains no such content. PSQ.7.b is the only standard in the
PSQ chapter that addresses sentinel-event definition requirements, and even
here the requirement is self-identification and documentation, not adherence
to a prescribed NABH category list.

Any draft for PSQ.7 that enumerates specific sentinel event types
(e.g., "wrong-site surgery", "retained instrument") is importing illustrative
examples from external frameworks. The source requires definition, not
compliance with any specific enumerated list.

This OE is asterisked — documentation of the sentinel-event list is required.

PSQ.7.c
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation has established processes for analysis of incidents.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF pp. 231–232):
The safety committee (Refer to PSQ.1.a.) shall be responsible for this
activity. This could preferably be done by identifying the root cause. Inputs
could be sought from the units/discipline/departments concerned. Where
possible, patients and other stakeholders could be included in analysing the
feedback and complaint.

The immediate response to a safety incident shall be to address the urgent
care and support needs of those involved. This shall not await analysis.

In case of sentinel events, correction if any shall be initiated within
24-working hours of occurrence or reporting. The analysis of sentinel events
shall be completed within seven working days of occurrence or reporting.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger as a formal NABH level.
However, the sentinel-event timelines are mandatory (shall) and time-bound —
they represent the strongest operational constraint in the PSQ chapter:

  SENTINEL EVENT TIME-BOUND OBLIGATIONS (both mandatory — shall):
  1. Correction, if any, shall be initiated within 24 working hours of
     occurrence or reporting.
  2. Analysis shall be completed within seven working days of occurrence or
     reporting.

These two frequencies are verbatim in the source and must be preserved
exactly — do not soften to "promptly", "quickly" or "as soon as possible".

Additional mandatory elements:
  — Safety committee (PSQ.1.a) shall be responsible for incident analysis.
  — Immediate response to address urgent care and support needs of those
    involved shall not await analysis — care continuity is unconditional.

Optional/preferred elements:
  — Root-cause analysis is preferred (could preferably) — not mandated.
  — Inputs from units/departments could be sought — illustrative.
  — Patient and stakeholder inclusion could be done where possible —
    illustrative.

PSQ.7.d
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Corrective and preventive actions are taken based on the findings of such
analysis.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 232):
The objective of this is to improve the quality of patient-care services
continually. All such action shall be documented. The findings and
recommendations arrived at after the analysis shall be communicated to all
personnel concerned to correct the systems and processes to prevent
recurrences. Any change in the policy or procedure is reflected as an
amendment in the organisation's documentation.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. Three mandatory (shall)
elements:
  1. All corrective and preventive actions shall be documented.
  2. Findings and recommendations shall be communicated to all personnel
     concerned.
  3. Any change in policy or procedure is reflected as an amendment in the
     organisation's documentation — this is stated without shall but in
     mandatory-declarative form ("is reflected").

CAPA without documentation is not this OE. Communication to only the
immediate team, not to "all personnel concerned", is not this OE.

PSQ.7.e
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation incorporates risks identified in the analysis of incidents
into the risk management system.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 232):
If the incident analysis reveals the presence of an unidentified risk, the
same shall be subjected to risk management.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no stop-work trigger. The trigger condition is
specific: an unidentified risk surfaced by incident analysis shall be
subjected to risk management. The OE is not about all risks — only risks that
incident analysis reveals as previously unidentified. No frequency or
timeframe is specified.

PSQ.7.f
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation shall have a process for informing various stakeholders in
case of a near miss / adverse event / sentinel event.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 232):
After due analysis, an incident could be termed as a near miss or adverse
event or sentinel event. Based on the nature of the near miss or adverse
event or sentinel event, the organisation shall inform the stakeholders
(including patient and family where applicable) regarding the relevant
concerns in addition to initiating the corrective and preventive action.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. One mandatory (shall)
requirement: the organisation shall inform stakeholders (including patient
and family where applicable) regarding the relevant concerns, based on the
nature of the classified incident.

Key constraints:
  — Stakeholder notification follows due analysis and incident classification
    — it is not a pre-analysis obligation (unlike PSQ.7.c immediate care
    response, which shall not await analysis).
  — "Including patient and family where applicable" — applicability is
    qualified; it is not unconditional.
  — The Guidebook does not prescribe notification to external regulatory or
    statutory authorities in PSQ.7.f. Statutory notification duties under
    applicable law are separate from this OE and are not enumerated here.
  — The process exists alongside CAPA, not as a replacement for it.

DRAFTING NOTE: PSQ.7.f does not authorise the drafter to enumerate a list of
event categories requiring external/regulatory reporting. Such a list would be
importing requirements from outside the NABH source. If an organisation has
statutory notification duties (e.g., to a state health authority for certain
events), those arise from law — not from PSQ.7.f.

SPECIAL NOTE: PSQ.7 IS THE ACTUAL SOURCE FOR SENTINEL-EVENT CONTENT IN PSQ
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A ChatGPT draft produced for PSQ.2 included content about reporting notifiable
and sentinel events, with what appeared to be an enumerated or appended list
of event categories. That content was not sourced from NABH.

In the HCO 6th Edition standard:

  1. "Sentinel events" is defined in the Glossary (PSQ.7.b cross-references
     this). The Glossary definition is the source; PSQ.7.b does not reproduce
     or expand it.

  2. PSQ.7.b is the ONLY OE in the PSQ chapter that requires the organisation
     to deal with sentinel-event definitions. It requires self-identification
     of relevant events and documentation of that self-identified list — not
     adherence to any NABH-prescribed category enumeration.

  3. PSQ.2 contains NO requirement, list, or reference relating to notifiable
     events or sentinel-event reporting categories. The nine OEs of PSQ.2
     (a–i) cover: QI committee, QI programme documentation, process
     efficiency, clinical appropriateness, designated QI coordinator, periodic
     review, annual update, internal audits, and nursing quality monitoring.

  4. PSQ.7.f requires a process to inform stakeholders (including patient and
     family where applicable) after incident classification. It does not
     prescribe a list of events triggering external statutory notification.
     Statutory reporting obligations arise from applicable law, not from
     PSQ.7.f.

  DRAFTING INSTRUCTION FOR ALL PSQ POLICIES: Any draft that enumerates
  specific sentinel event categories, or references notifiable-event lists as
  NABH requirements, is fabricating content. The correct approach is:
    — Generic reference to the organisation's own documented sentinel-event
      list (PSQ.7.b obligation).
    — Generic reference to the organisation's obligation to inform
      stakeholders after classification (PSQ.7.f obligation).
    — No NABH-sourced enumerated list of sentinel or notifiable event
      categories exists within the PSQ chapter standards text.
