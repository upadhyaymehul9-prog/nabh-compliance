Uniform care to patients is provided in all settings of the organisation and is guided

COP.1.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Uniform care is provided to patients following written guidance.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 100):
The organisation needs to implement mechanisms to ensure that the care, the patient
receives in out-patient/day care/in-patient/emergency is uniform. Uniform patient care
is linked to access, appropriateness of care and treatment. It does not depend on the
patient's ability to pay or the source of payment. The level of care provided to patients
shall be the same throughout the organisation.

Care delivery is uniform for a given clinical condition when similar care is provided in
more than one setting. When similar treatment/care is provided in various settings such
as out-patients, day care, in-patients, and different categories of wards, the
organisation shall ensure that patients with the same clinical condition and care needs
receive the same quality of health care throughout the organisation. Care delivery shall
be applicable irrespective of the setting and category of the ward, and whether the
patient is paying or non-paying, and/or is supported from a governmental/private
insurance scheme or not. For example, the decision to offer any form of intervention or
medication, frequency of consultant visit, nature of support care, decision to discharge
is not influenced by the class or category of the patient but is decided by the clinical
needs of the patient. Further, in case the organisation has separate OPDs for different
category of patients, the methodology for care delivery shall be uniform in all OPDs.

[Guidebook cross-ref: AAC.4, AAC.5, PRE.4 — in policy draft: "those requirements are
covered in the hospital's other policies."]

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. COP.1 not in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS.

Mandatory (shall):
  — The level of care shall be the same throughout the organisation.
  — Care delivery shall apply irrespective of setting, ward category, and payment status.
  — Where the same clinical condition is treated in multiple settings, uniformity across
    those settings is required.

Illustrative only (not a mandatory checklist):
  — Specific examples (OPD categories, consultant visit frequency, discharge decision,
    intervention/medication offers) illustrate uniformity — they are "for example" items.
  — The mechanism for ensuring uniformity is for the organisation to determine.

Documentation: OE is starred — the written guidance operationalising uniform care must
be documented.

COP.1.b
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation has a uniform process for identification of patients and uses at least
two identifiers.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 100):
The mechanism for identification of patients shall be uniform across the organisation.
For example, the use of ID bands with the patient's name and unique identification
number. The same shall be used in imaging, diagnostic and therapeutic procedures,
blood transfusion, day care and nutrition/diet. For any care related aspect, at a
minimum, two identifiers shall be used. One of the identifiers shall be the unique
identification number generated at the time of registration.

[Guidebook cross-ref: AAC.2.b — in policy draft: "those requirements are covered in
the hospital's other policies."]

Stop-work check: CORE — non-compliance is accreditation-blocking. COP.1 has no
designated stop-work authority section. CORE status means the process must be present
and functional; it does not create a stop-work trigger clause in this policy.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Identification mechanism shall be uniform across the organisation.
  2. At a minimum, two identifiers shall be used for any care-related aspect —
     exact minimum: two. Preserve verbatim; do not soften.
  3. One identifier shall be the unique identification number generated at registration —
     mandatory (shall); registration-number is specified as a required identifier.
  4. The same process shall apply in imaging, diagnostic and therapeutic procedures,
     blood transfusion, day care, and nutrition/diet — mandatory scope breadth.

Illustrative only:
  — ID bands with name and unique ID number are one example of a mechanism —
    the specific mechanism (wristband, card, etc.) is the organisation's choice.
  — The second identifier (beyond registration number) is the organisation's to select.

Verbatim OE text:
Multi-disciplinary and multi-speciality care, where appropriate, is planned based on
best clinical practices / clinical practice guidelines and delivered in a uniform manner

Whenever the patient's clinical condition warrants care from multiple disciplines for
example doctors, nurses, rehabilitation specialist, dietician, psychologist, etc and/or

Some examples of scenarios where multidisciplinary care plan is required are care of
a cancer patient to be determined by a multi-disciplinary tumour board; care of
transplant patients; diabetes patient to be cared for by multiple specialists to prevent
and manage end-organ damage; palliative/end-of-life care to be provided to terminally
ill patients; long-stay patients.

the hospital's other policies."]

  — An integrated care plan shall be developed and implemented by representatives of

Verbatim OE text:
There shall be an identified area in the organisation which is easily accessible to
receive and manage emergency patients, with adequate and appropriate resources.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 102):
The identified area to treat emergency patients shall be easily accessible for the
initiation of care. There shall be signage and directions in an understandable manner
leading to the emergency area of the organisation. Emergency services preferably have
designated triage, resuscitation treatment and patient holding areas identified. The
organisation shall also specify the minimum number of beds based on its scope of
services. At a minimum, adequate and appropriate human resource, basic resuscitation
equipment, equipment for monitoring vital parameters, appropriate consumables and
life-saving and emergency care drugs shall be available.

and have access to ongoing training programme.

"those requirements are covered in the hospital's other policies."]

of policy). The OE itself is Commitment level; the stop-work authority draws on the
combined failure of identified area + triage competence + basic equipment.

Mandatory (shall):
  — There shall be an identified area easily accessible — mandatory existence (shall).
  — Signage and directions in understandable manner shall be present (shall).
  — The organisation shall specify the minimum number of beds per scope (shall).
  — At a minimum: adequate human resource, basic resuscitation equipment, vital-
    parameter monitoring equipment, consumables, and life-saving/emergency drugs
    shall be available (shall) — this minimum resource list is mandatory.
  — Personnel shall be privileged to work in the area and have access to ongoing
    training (shall).

Illustrative:

COP.2 Stop-work Authority (from STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS / hco_cop_v2_common.py)

Internal reason: "Emergency overcrowding / missing triage competence / dead-on-arrival
pathway gaps"

Hospital-facing text (verbatim from source):
Do not receive and treat an emergency patient in an area that is not the identified
emergency area, or when triage-competent personnel and basic resuscitation equipment
are not available.

Do not leave overcrowding unmanaged when it blocks triage, resuscitation or safe
holding.

Stop-work applies to unsafe emergency intake, not to immediate life-saving measures
already under way — those continue while escalation happens.

The person who stops tells the Emergency In-Charge and the Medical Superintendent
the same shift. Refusing unsafe emergency intake is not a disciplinary matter.

COP.3 — Ambulance Services and Safe Transportation
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Standard text (verbatim):
Ambulance services ensure safe patient transportation with appropriate care.

OE count: 7 | COREs: none | Stars: f★
Stop-work: YES | Statute disclaimer: YES
Prepared by: Ambulance / Transport In-Charge | Doc no: HCO/COP/POL/03


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.3.a
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation has access to ambulance services commensurate with the scope of the
services provided by it.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 105):
Commensurate to its scope of services, the organisation may provide in-house or use
outsourced ambulance service for safe patient transport with appropriate care.

The organisation shall decide the appropriate level of an ambulance to be provided
based on the National Ambulance Code AIS-125.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger from this OE alone. Feeds into
the COP.3 stop-work trigger when readiness conditions are absent.

Mandatory (shall):
  — The organisation shall decide the appropriate ambulance level per National
    Ambulance Code AIS-125 — mandatory (shall); AIS-125 is the reference standard.
  — Access (in-house or outsourced) is required — the form of access is the
    organisation's choice (may).

External reference: National Ambulance Code AIS-125 — reference only; do not
enumerate AIS-125 clauses as NABH requirements.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.3.b
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
There are adequate access and space for the ambulance(s).

Guidebook interpretation (PDF pp. 105–106):
The organisation shall demarcate a proper space for the ambulance(s). The demarcation
shall be done keeping in mind easy accessibility for receiving patients and enabling
the ambulance(s) to exit quickly. Adequate and prominent signage shall exist to guide
the ambulance drivers to the ambulance entry and route to the emergency department.
It is preferable that the organisation has an ambulance parking bay.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger from this OE alone.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. A proper space shall be demarcated (shall).
  2. Demarcation shall enable easy accessibility for receiving patients and quick exit
     (shall).
  3. Adequate and prominent signage shall exist guiding drivers to ambulance entry
     and emergency route (shall).

Illustrative:
  — Ambulance parking bay is "preferable" — preferred, not mandatory.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.3.c
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The ambulance(s) is fit for purpose and is appropriately equipped.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 106):
The vehicle used as an ambulance shall adhere to statutory requirements for example
registration as an ambulance under the Motor Vehicle Act, valid fitness certificate,
pollution control certificate, and insurance of the vehicle.

An ambulance shall have at least basic life support equipment, for both adult and
paediatric patients. Based on the organisation's scope, additional equipment, for
example monitoring and resuscitative equipment, shall be available in the ambulance.

Reference: National Ambulance Code AIS-125.

Stop-work check: Commitment — feeds into the COP.3 stop-work trigger (non-functional
equipment is a stop-work condition).

Mandatory (shall):
  1. The vehicle shall adhere to statutory requirements — mandatory, governed by law.
     The listed examples (Motor Vehicle Act registration, fitness certificate, pollution
     control, insurance) are examples of statutory requirements — not an exhaustive
     list substituting for the applicable law.
  2. At least basic life support equipment for adult and paediatric patients shall be
     available — exact minimum (shall).
  3. Additional equipment based on scope shall be available (shall — scope-dependent).

Statutory disclaimer: Vehicle compliance is governed by applicable laws (Motor Vehicle
Act and related regulations) — these are statutory requirements, not NABH-prescribed
checklists.

COP.3.d
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The ambulance(s) is operated by trained personnel.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 106):
The ambulance shall be operated by a driver with a valid driving licence. Additionally,
technician/nurse and/or doctor may be there depending on the situation and scope of
the ambulance. Personnel in the ambulance shall have training in basic life support and
be trained in basic cardiopulmonary resuscitation.

Stop-work check: Commitment — feeds into the COP.3 stop-work trigger (driver not
licensed for vehicle class is a stop-work condition).

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Driver shall have a valid driving licence — mandatory (shall).
  2. All ambulance personnel shall have training in basic life support and basic CPR —
     mandatory (shall); applies to whoever is in the ambulance.

Illustrative:
  — Technician, nurse, and/or doctor presence "may" depend on situation and scope —
    optional additional crew beyond the driver; the organisation determines based on
    scope.

COP.3.e
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The ambulance(s) is checked daily for functioning status, medical equipment, emergency
medications and consumables.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 106):
The check shall indicate the functioning status of the ambulance like lights, siren,
beacon lights, fuel, tyres etc. The checks shall indicate the functioning status of the
medical equipment based on a documented checklist. Emergency medications are available
in the ambulance during patient transport. Based on a checklist, a daily check is done
to ensure availability and expiry dates of emergency medications and documented. After
the return from every trip, medications used shall be topped up if used and the same
verified and documented. The medications shall be stored in a safe environment as per
policy of the organisation. In case the organisation follows a system of sealing the
emergency medication kit, then the check shall be carried out after each use of the kit.

Stop-work check: Commitment — feeds into the COP.3 stop-work trigger (non-functional
equipment is a stop-work condition; the daily check is the mechanism for verifying
readiness before dispatch).

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Daily check shall be done and documented — mandatory frequency and documentation.
  2. Check shall cover functioning status of the vehicle (lights, siren, beacon,
     fuel, tyres — these are examples of vehicle systems, not an exclusive list).
  3. Medical equipment functioning status shall be checked per a documented checklist.
  4. Emergency medication availability and expiry dates shall be checked daily and
     documented.
  5. After each trip, medications used shall be topped up, verified, and documented
     (shall — post-trip obligation, not just daily).
  6. Medications shall be stored per the organisation's policy (shall).
  7. Where sealed emergency medication kits are used, the check shall be after each
     use of the kit.

DRAFTING NOTE: The daily check and post-trip top-up are two separate mandatory
obligations — both must appear in the policy. The daily check is not sufficient alone.

COP.3.f
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The ambulance(s) has a proper communication system.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 107):
The ambulance shall be connected with the organisation/control room by wireless/mobile

patient transport is received, who are the people expected to respond and organise the

Stop-work check: Commitment — feeds directly into the COP.3 stop-work trigger

Mandatory (shall):

  2. The communication system shall encompass the whole transport process (shall).
  3. Written guidance shall exist covering: how a transport call is received; who
     responds and organises transport (shall).
  4. The ambulance shall leave within a predefined timeframe based on the patient's
     needs (shall) — the specific timeframe is for the organisation to define based on
     patient need; no NABH-prescribed number of minutes is stated.

Documentation: OE is starred — the written guidance for the communication process is
required documentation.

COP.3.g
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The emergency department identifies opportunities to initiate treatment at the earliest
when the patient is in transit to the organisation.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 107):
From the time of first communication with the patient/patient's attendant, a file is
created to record appropriate information, attempts are made to gather important
clinical information (patient's age, weight, provisional diagnosis and ongoing
treatment at the referring organisation). This information is used by the ambulance
personnel of the receiving organisation to be better prepared to assess, initiate
emergency care/interventions during transit and transport the patient safely.

During the transit, when required, there is an exchange of information between the
ambulance personnel and the medical professional at the receiving organisation. The
information will help the doctor at the receiving organisation guide the ambulance
personnel to facilitate the management during the transit.

When the patient is being shifted by an external agency, wherever possible, an attempt
is made by the doctor of the receiving organisation to communicate with the ambulance
personnel of the external agency to ascertain the clinical situation and make
appropriate suggestions. However, the medical professional in the ambulance would be
responsible for decision-making regarding the care/interventions during the transit.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no stop-work trigger.

Key operational elements (descriptive — no explicit "shall" for most steps):
  — A file shall be created from first communication to record clinical information
    (the Guidebook uses "is created" declaratively — treat as operational expectation).
  — Clinical information gathered: age, weight, provisional diagnosis, ongoing
    treatment — these are examples of what to gather, not an exhaustive mandatory list.
  — Exchange of information during transit is expected "when required" — clinically
    triggered.
  — When shifted by external agency: the receiving doctor attempts to communicate
    "wherever possible" — qualified, not absolute.
  — Medical professional in the ambulance is responsible for in-transit care decisions
    regardless of who provides the ambulance — this is a clear responsibility allocation.

DRAFTING NOTE: This is an Achievement-level OE that describes a pre-hospital care
readiness process. The language is largely declarative rather than prescriptive. Draft
as operational practice without inventing mandatory timeframes or information elements
beyond what the source states.

COP.3 Stop-work Authority (from STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS / hco_cop_v2_common.py)

Internal reason: "Ambulance departure without functioning equipment, competent crew,
or communication link"

Hospital-facing text (verbatim from source):
Do not dispatch or continue an ambulance transfer when the vehicle fails its readiness
check, required equipment is non-functional, the driver is not licensed for the vehicle
class, or the organisation/control-room communication link is down.

Stop-work applies to the transfer dispatch, not to on-scene life-saving measures.

The person who stops tells the Ambulance / Transport In-Charge and the Medical
Superintendent the same shift. Refusing an unsafe ambulance dispatch is not a
disciplinary matter.

COP.4 — Community Emergencies, Epidemics and Disasters
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Standard text (verbatim):
The organisation plans and implements mechanisms for the care of patients during
community emergencies, epidemics and other disasters.

OE count: 4 | COREs: none | Stars: a★, b★
No stop-work authority | No statute disclaimer
Prepared by: Medical Superintendent | Doc no: HCO/COP/POL/04


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.4.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation identifies potential community emergencies, epidemics and other
disasters.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 108):
The organisation shall identify potential community emergencies, epidemics and other
disasters likely to cause a sudden rush of victims. Some examples include earthquake,
floods, train accident, civil unrest outside the organisation's premises, major fire
and outbreak of disease/epidemics.

These shall be identified based on geographical location and the community served by
the organisation. For example, an organisation in an industrial town shall identify the
industrial hazards that may occur in its vicinity.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  — The organisation shall identify potential community emergencies, epidemics and
    disasters based on geographical location and community served (shall).

Illustrative:
  — The specific emergencies listed (earthquake, floods, train accident, civil unrest,
    major fire, disease outbreak, industrial hazards) are examples of what may be
    relevant — not a mandatory enumerated list. The organisation identifies what is
    applicable to its geography and community.

Documentation: OE is starred — the identification process and its output (the
identified list) must be documented.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.4.b
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation manages community emergencies, epidemics and other disasters as per
a documented plan.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 108):
The disaster, community emergency and epidemic plan must incorporate essential
elements of alert code, information and communication, action cards for each of the
staff, availability and earmarking of resources including adequacy of medical supplies,
equipment, materials, trained personnel, establishment of command nucleus, training
and mock drills, and managing clinical activities during the event. The emergency room
could follow triage policy according to the National Disaster Management Authority
(NDMA) guidelines.

It shall also include aspects like activating and deactivating plan; receive, identify
and triage casualties; defined areas for reception and treatment for casualties;
transportation aids; communication aids; manage visitors, and control the movement of
individuals and vehicles, relocate/discharge admitted patients wherever needed.

The plans shall conform to the relevant local laws and national plans on disaster
management. A good reference is NDMA guidelines.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. A documented plan shall exist.
  2. The plan must incorporate (mandatory content — "must"):
     — Alert code
     — Information and communication systems
     — Action cards for each staff member
     — Availability and earmarking of resources (medical supplies, equipment,
        materials, trained personnel)
     — Establishment of command nucleus
     — Training and mock drills
     — Managing clinical activities during the event
  3. The plan shall also include (mandatory — "shall include"):
     — Activating and deactivating the plan
     — Receive, identify and triage casualties
     — Defined areas for reception and treatment of casualties
     — Transportation aids
     — Communication aids
     — Managing visitors and movement of individuals and vehicles
     — Relocating/discharging admitted patients wherever needed
  4. Plans shall conform to relevant local laws and national disaster management plans
     (shall).

NOTE: The two lists above are both mandatory content elements of the documented plan
— "must incorporate" and "shall also include" both carry obligatory force. Preserve
all elements from both lists in the policy.

Illustrative:
  — Triage per NDMA guidelines in the ER "could" — optional, not mandatory.
  — NDMA guidelines are "a good reference" — not a prescribed compliance document.

Documentation: OE is starred — the documented plan is the required documentation.

Provision is made for availability of medical supplies, equipment and materials during
such emergencies.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 108):
Resource availability shall be according to threat perception. The number of resources
i.e. medical consumables, equipment, etc. to be commensurate with the expected

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

COP.4.d
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The plan is tested at least twice a year.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 109):
Testing twice a year is only the minimum frequency, and this may be increased. In case
the organisation has different plans for different disasters, each of the plans shall be
tested at least twice a year.

The plan can be tested using a table-top exercise, or a mock drill. At a minimum, at
least one mock drill shall be held once in 12 months. This shall test all the
components of the plan and not just awareness. In the case of a mock drill, simulated
patients (not real) shall be used. After every table-top exercise/mock drill, the
variations are identified, the reason for the same is analysed, debriefing conducted
and where appropriate the necessary corrective and/or preventive actions are taken.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

TWO MANDATORY FREQUENCIES — PRESERVE BOTH VERBATIM:
  1. The plan shall be tested at least twice a year — mandatory minimum (shall).
     Where multiple disaster-type plans exist, each plan shall be tested at least
     twice a year (shall).
  2. At least one mock drill shall be held once in 12 months — this is a separate
     additional obligation within the testing requirement (shall). The overall testing
     frequency is twice a year; within that, at least one event must be a mock drill
     (not a table-top exercise).

Additional mandatory elements:
  — Testing shall cover all components of the plan, not just awareness — this is a
    content-of-test obligation (shall).
  — Mock drills shall use simulated patients, not real patients (shall).
  — After every exercise/drill: variations identified, reason analysed, debriefing
    conducted, corrective/preventive actions taken where appropriate (shall — all four
    post-test steps).

Illustrative:
  — Testing "may" be increased beyond twice a year — permitted, not mandated.
  — Table-top exercise or mock drill — both are acceptable methods; the organisation
    chooses, subject to the mock-drill minimum.
