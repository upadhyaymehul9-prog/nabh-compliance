## Statement of intent

There is an established system for clinical audit.

## 1. Purpose

This policy says how Preview Hospital meets NABH Hospitals 6th Edition standard PSQ.5: There is an established system for clinical audit.

It covers objective elements PSQ.5.a–f (6 elements).

Chapter intent (official Standards PDF): The standards encourage an environment of patient safety and continual quality improvement. The patient safety and quality programme should be documented and involve all areas of the organisation and all staff members. The management creates a culture of safety in the organisation. Patient safety officer(s) shall be designated for the implementation of patient-safety programme. National / International patient-safety goals / solutions / framework shall be implemented. The organisation shall collect data on structures, processes and outcomes, especially in areas of high-risk situations. Quality indicators specific to clinical specialities shall be used to involve clinical departments in the quality improvement programme. The collected data shall be collated, analysed and trends are used for further improvement. Appropriate quality tools shall be used for carrying out quality improvement projects. Clinical audits shall be used as a tool to improve the quality of patient care in a sustained manner. Department leaders play an active role in patient safety and quality improvement. The organisation has a mechanism to capture patient reported outcome measures. The organisation shall have a robust incident reporting system. Sentinel events shall be defined. All incidents are investigated and appropriate action is taken. The management shall support the patient safety and quality programme.

This policy owns PSQ.5. Related AAC, COP, MOM, PRE and IPC duties stay with those policies — cross-reference only. Other PSQ standards stay with their own policies.

## 5. What we do

### 5.1 Clinical audits are performed to improve the quality of patient care

Clinical audits are performed to improve the quality of patient care, not only for documentation completeness and not as research projects.

The «Quality Improvement Committee» keeps a clinical-audit calendar. At a minimum the organisation conducts one clinical audit per clinical department once in two years, as per the scope of services. Topics may be disease-based, cost-based, community-based or morbidity-based (length of stay). Completed reports are filed.

Method note (from guidebook interpretation): The organisation shall use clinical audits as a quality improvement tool to improve the quality of patient care. The clinical audit could be retrospective/prospective in nature. The topic for audit could be disease-based, cost-based, community-based or based on morbidity (length of stay). The organisation shall conduct one clinical audit per clinical department once in two years as per the scope of services. The organisation needs to take care to differentiate clinical audit from research projects. — Excellence g G _ b. ©:

### 5.2 The parameters to be audited are defined by the organisation

Parameters to be audited are defined in writing before data collection: objectives, standards/criteria, sample, period and exclusions.

The audit lead agrees parameters with the «Quality Coordinator». An audit started without predefined parameters is stopped and restarted against agreed criteria.

Method note (from guidebook interpretation): As clinical audits are standards-based, they must be done using predefined parameters so that there is no bias. The organisation shall lay down the objectives, the standards against which the audit will be conducted, develop a checklist where required, sampling and data collection guidelines and preparation of the report. The audit shall encompass aspects of clinical and nursing care.

### 5.3 Medical and nursing staff participate in clinical audit

Medical and nursing staff participate in clinical audit: as audit leads, data collectors or in the results discussion for their specialty.

Attendance and role are recorded. An audit done only by the quality office with no clinical participation is not this Achievement element.

Method note (from guidebook interpretation): The organisation shall identify such personnel. It could be a mix of clinicians, administrators and nurses. These could be members of the core committee / quality assurance committee.

### 5.4 Patient and staff anonymity are maintained

Patient and staff anonymity are maintained in clinical-audit reports and presentations. Identifiers are removed or coded; the key is held separately by the «Quality Coordinator».

Reports used in open meetings do not name patients or staff. A named case discussion is a morbidity meeting, not this audit report.

Method note (from guidebook interpretation): This means that the names of the patients and the hospital staff who may figure in the audit documents must not be disclosed nor any reference is made to them in public discussions/conferences. This is at the stage of report preparation and dissemination. The staff participating in the audit shall maintain patient and staff anonymity and not reveal names.

### 5.5 Clinical audits are documented

Clinical audits are documented: protocol/parameters, data, findings, recommendations, attendees and date.

The «Quality Coordinator» files the pack. An undocumented verbal audit is not this element.

Method note (from guidebook interpretation): The organisation could use a checklist with the predefined parameters, and the audit findings could be recorded on this sheet. After the audit, a report shall be prepared, highlighting the key findings of the audit.

### 5.6 Remedial measures are implemented

Remedial measures from clinical audit are implemented: each recommendation has an owner, due date and a later check that it was done.

The «Quality Improvement Committee» reviews open actions. A report without followed-up remedies is incomplete under this element.

Method note (from guidebook interpretation): All remedial measures as ascertained shall be documented and implemented, and improvements thereof recorded to complete the audit cycle. This shall preferably be done based on the root-cause analysis.

## 6. Governance and responsibility

Medical Superintendent
- Accountable that PSQ.5 is resourced and followed.
- Acts on sentinel events, safety-culture failures and quality-programme gaps that reach top leadership.

Quality Coordinator
- Owns day-to-day implementation and records for this standard.

Patient Safety Officer
- Coordinates the patient-safety programme and incident/sentinel-event path across standards.

Quality Coordinator
- Coordinates the quality-improvement programme, indicators, audits and projects as this standard requires.
- Audits this policy «quarterly»; holds training acknowledgements.

Nursing Superintendent
- Owns nursing-care quality monitoring where this standard requires it.

## 7. Quality monitoring (RCA → CAPA)

The Quality Coordinator audits this policy «quarterly».

What is monitored each quarter:

- Sample of records for each OE PSQ.5.a–f.
- Asterisked elements (none) have document evidence as required.
- CORE elements (none) show no critical gaps in the sample.
- Stop-work events (if any) are logged with outcome.

This policy is reviewed «annually», and sooner after a related sentinel event, indicator failure cluster or failed clinical-audit cycle.
