Standard text (verbatim):
Uniform care to patients is provided in all settings of the organisation and is guided by

Standard is starred (★).

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.1.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Uniform care is provided to patients following written guidance.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 100):
The organisation needs to implement mechanisms to ensure that the care, the patient
receives in out-patient/day care/in-patient/emergency is uniform. Uniform patient care
is linked to access, appropriateness of care and treatment. It does not depend on the
patient's ability to pay or the source of payment. The level of care provided to patients
shall be the same throughout the organisation.

Care delivery is uniform for a given clinical condition when similar care is provided in
more than one setting. When similar treatment/care is provided in various settings such
as out-patients, day care, in-patients, and different categories of wards, the
organisation shall ensure that patients with the same clinical condition and care needs
receive the same quality of health care throughout the organisation. Care delivery shall
be applicable irrespective of the setting and category of the ward, and whether the
patient is paying or non-paying, and/or is supported from a governmental/private
insurance scheme or not. For example, the decision to offer any form of intervention or
medication, frequency of consultant visit, nature of support care, decision to discharge
is not influenced by the class or category of the patient but is decided by the clinical
needs of the patient. Further, in case the organisation has separate OPDs for different
category of patients, the methodology for care delivery shall be uniform in all OPDs.

Refer to AAC.4., AAC.5. and PRE.4.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. COP.1 is not in
STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS (those are COP.2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 16).

Mandatory elements:
  — The level of care provided to patients shall be the same throughout the organisation
    (shall).
  — Care delivery shall be applicable irrespective of setting, ward category, and
    payment status (shall).
  — Where the same clinical condition is treated in multiple settings, uniformity across
    those settings is required (shall).

Non-mandatory (illustrative):
  — The specific examples (OPD categories, consultant visit frequency, discharge
    decision basis, intervention/medication offers) illustrate what uniformity means —
    they are not a mandatory enumerated checklist.
  — The mechanism for ensuring uniformity is for the organisation to determine.

Documentation:
  This OE is starred — the written guidance that ensures uniform care must be documented.
  The star is on the OE, not on any specific mechanism; the organisation documents whatever
  guidance it uses to operationalise uniform care.

Cross-references: AAC.4 (care planning), AAC.5 (care planning documentation),
PRE.4 (equitable patient rights).

COP.1.b
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation has a uniform process for identification of patients and uses at least
two identifiers.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 100):
The mechanism for identification of patients shall be uniform across the organisation.
For example, the use of ID bands with the patient's name and unique identification
number. The same shall be used in imaging, diagnostic and therapeutic procedures,
blood transfusion, day care and nutrition/diet. For any care related aspect, at a
minimum, two identifiers shall be used. One of the identifiers shall be the unique
identification number generated at the time of registration.

Refer to AAC.2.b.

Stop-work check: CORE — absence of a uniform patient identification process with a
minimum of two identifiers is CORE non-compliance and accreditation-blocking. However,
COP.1 has no designated stop-work authority section (stop-work authority exists for
COP.2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 16 only). The CORE status means it must be present
and functional — not that a stop-work trigger exists in the policy.

Three mandatory (shall) elements:
  1. The identification mechanism shall be uniform across the organisation.
  2. At a minimum, two identifiers shall be used for any care-related aspect — this is
     an exact minimum (two), not advisory.
  3. One of the identifiers shall be the unique identification number generated at
     the time of registration — mandatory (shall); which identifier is assigned to
     registration-number is specified.
  4. The same process shall be used in imaging, diagnostic and therapeutic procedures,
     blood transfusion, day care and nutrition/diet — mandatory scope breadth.

Non-mandatory (illustrative):
  — ID bands with name and unique ID number are given as an example — the specific
    mechanism (wristband, card, sticker) is the organisation's choice.
  — The second identifier (beyond registration number) is for the organisation to
    select, provided it is consistent and reliable.

Cross-reference: AAC.2.b.

COP.1.c
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation implements evidence-based clinical practice guidelines and/or clinical
protocols to guide uniform patient care.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 101):
Clinical practice guidelines brought out by National and International professional
organisations may be used. Standard treatment guidelines (STGs) brought out by the
Government of India are a good starting point. In the absence of evidence-based clinical
practice guidelines or where adapting the clinical practice guidelines are not feasible,
sound clinical practices shall guide the delivery of care.

For definitions of "evidence-based medicine" and "clinical practice guidelines", refer
to the glossary.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

Mandatory element:
  — Where evidence-based CPGs are unavailable or adaptation is not feasible, sound
    clinical practices shall guide care — this establishes a mandatory fallback (shall).
  — The OE itself uses "implements" — adoption and use of CPGs and/or protocols is
    required, not optional.

Non-mandatory (illustrative):
  — National and International professional organisation CPGs may be used — the
    source uses "may", meaning these are permissible but not the exclusive source.
  — Government of India STGs are described as "a good starting point" — recommended,
    not prescribed.
  — The organisation selects appropriate CPGs for its own scope of services; no specific
    CPG titles or clinical topics are mandated by the OE or Guidebook.

Definitions: "evidence-based medicine" and "clinical practice guidelines" are in the
Glossary — do not fabricate or paraphrase these in the policy body.

COP.1.d
Level: Excellence | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Clinical care pathways are developed, consistently followed across all settings of care,
and reviewed periodically.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 101):
Clinical care pathways shall be multidisciplinary, and based on the evidence and/or
best clinical practices which provides detailed guidance at various stages of care. At
a minimum the organisation shall develop two clinical care pathways every year. The
clinical care pathways shall be reviewed and updated annually.

Stop-work check: Excellence — no stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall) elements:
  1. Pathways shall be multidisciplinary.
  2. Pathways shall be based on evidence and/or best clinical practices.
  3. At a minimum, two clinical care pathways shall be developed every year — exact
     minimum quantity: two per year. Preserve this verbatim; do not soften to "some"
     or "a number of".
  4. Pathways shall be reviewed and updated annually — exact mandatory frequency.

Non-mandatory:
  — The specific clinical topics for the pathways are for the organisation to select.
  — "Detailed guidance at various stages of care" describes the required depth of a
    pathway, not a mandatory template format.

COP.1.e
Level: Excellence | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Multi-disciplinary and multi-speciality care, where appropriate, is planned based on
best clinical practices / clinical practice guidelines and delivered in a uniform manner
across the organisation.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 101):
Whenever the patient's clinical condition warrants care from multiple disciplines for
example doctors, nurses, rehabilitation specialist, dietician, psychologist, etc and/or
multiple specialties for example cardiology, endocrinology, surgery, internal medicine;
multidisciplinary and multi-speciality care plan shall be charted out based on
established best clinical practices/clinical practice guidelines for the specific
clinical condition. An integrated care plan, including medical professional, nursing,
nutritional, supportive care professionals, shall be developed and implemented
appropriately by the representatives of various disciplines and specialities.

Some examples of scenarios where multidisciplinary care plan is required are care of
a cancer patient to be determined by a multi-disciplinary tumour board; care of
transplant patients; diabetes patient to be cared for by multiple specialists to prevent
and manage end-organ damage; palliative/end-of-life care to be provided to terminally
ill patients; long-stay patients.

Refer to PRE.3.e.

Stop-work check: Excellence — no stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall) elements, triggered by clinical need:
  — When clinical condition warrants multi-disciplinary/multi-speciality involvement,
    the care plan shall be charted out based on established best clinical
    practices/CPGs — mandatory (shall).
  — An integrated care plan shall be developed and implemented by representatives of
    the relevant disciplines and specialities — mandatory (shall).

Trigger condition: "where appropriate" / "whenever the patient's clinical condition
warrants" — the OE is clinically triggered, not blanket. The policy must reflect this
conditionality — not state that all patients get multi-disciplinary plans regardless
of clinical need.

Non-mandatory (illustrative):
  — The examples of disciplines (doctors, nurses, rehabilitation, dietician,
    psychologist) and specialties (cardiology, endocrinology, surgery, internal
    medicine) are illustrative, not an exhaustive or mandatory composition list.
  — The clinical scenarios (cancer patient, transplant, diabetes, palliative, long-stay)
    are examples of when multi-disciplinary planning is triggered — not a closed list
    of the only situations requiring it.

Cross-reference: PRE.3.e.

COP.1.f
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Telemedicine facility is provided safely and securely based on written guidance.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 102):
Whenever the telemedicine facility is provided, the organisation shall develop written
guidance in consonance with prevailing laws and guidelines and implement the same. The
written guidance shall include protection of the patient's identity and confidentiality.
The limitations of information and communication technologies shall be explicitly
addressed. The organisation shall have a mechanism for appropriate data storage and
retrieval.

The organisation shall have an MoU for its telemedicine services if outsourced.

Reference: Telemedicine Practice ICMR Guidelines, 2020, MoHFW.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger.

CONDITIONAL OE: This OE applies only where the organisation provides telemedicine
services. The Guidebook uses "whenever the telemedicine facility is provided" as the
applicability condition. If the organisation does not offer telemedicine, this OE does
not generate requirements, but the policy must state the applicability condition
explicitly rather than omitting the OE entirely.

Mandatory (shall) elements where telemedicine is provided:
  1. Written guidance shall be developed in consonance with prevailing laws and
     guidelines and implemented — mandatory (shall); guidance must be active, not
     merely drafted.
  2. Written guidance shall include protection of patient identity and confidentiality —
     mandatory content element (shall).
  3. ICT limitations shall be explicitly addressed in the written guidance — mandatory
     (shall); "explicitly" is the source word, meaning a general statement of
     confidentiality does not satisfy this.
  4. A mechanism for appropriate data storage and retrieval shall exist — mandatory
     (shall).
  5. An MoU shall exist if telemedicine services are outsourced — mandatory where
     applicable.

Non-mandatory:
  — The Telemedicine Practice ICMR Guidelines, 2020, MoHFW, are referenced as an
    external source — the organisation is guided by them but the OE does not
    explicitly mandate compliance with any specific clause of that document. The policy
    may reference the guidelines but should not enumerate specific clauses as
    mandatory NABH requirements.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the written guidance is the required documentation.

DRAFTING NOTES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. TWO EXACT QUANTITIES TO PRESERVE VERBATIM:
   — COP.1.b: "at least two identifiers" — do not weaken to "multiple" or "adequate"
   — COP.1.d: "at least two clinical care pathways every year" — do not weaken to
     "a number of" or "some"

2. TWO EXACT FREQUENCIES TO PRESERVE VERBATIM:
   — COP.1.d: "reviewed and updated annually"
   (No other mandatory frequency in this standard)

3. CONDITIONAL OE (COP.1.f): The policy must include an applicability statement for
   COP.1.f: "This section applies where [Hospital Name] provides telemedicine services."
   Do not write COP.1.f requirements as unconditional obligations applying to all
   hospitals.

4. ILLUSTRATION vs. OBLIGATION:
   — COP.1.a: the examples of uniformity (OPD categories, discharge decisions, etc.)
     are "for example" illustrations, not a mandatory audit checklist.
   — COP.1.c: no specific CPG titles are mandated; the organisation selects appropriate
     ones for its scope.
   — COP.1.e: the lists of disciplines and clinical scenarios are illustrative, not
     exhaustive or mandatory compositions.
   — COP.1.f: Telemedicine Practice ICMR Guidelines, 2020, are a reference source —
     do not enumerate specific clauses as mandatory NABH requirements.

5. EXCELLENCE LEVEL (COP.1.d, COP.1.e): These OEs are Excellence level — the highest
   tier. The policy should cover them but should not present them as basic NABH
   compliance elements at the same weight as CORE or Commitment OEs. The traceability
   table and checklist should note the Excellence level.

6. CROSS-REFERENCES in the Guidebook:
   — AAC.4 and AAC.5: care planning (COP.1.a)
   — PRE.4: patient rights, equitable care (COP.1.a)
   — AAC.2.b: patient identification (COP.1.b)
   — PRE.3.e: multi-disciplinary care (COP.1.e)
   These should appear as cross-references in traceability or scope, not reproduced
   as COP.1 obligations.
