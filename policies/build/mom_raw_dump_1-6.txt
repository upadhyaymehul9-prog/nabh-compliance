CHAPTER INTENT (official Standards PDF, verbatim):
The organisation has a safe and organised medication management process. Availability,
safe storage, prescription, dispensing and administration of medications are governed by
written guidance. A medication safety officer is designated. The hospital formulary is
developed, implemented and updated. Pharmacy has oversight of medications stocked out of
the pharmacy. Reconciliation occurs at transition points. Patients are monitored after
administration. Near misses, medication errors and adverse drug reactions are reported and
analysed. Medications also include blood, implants and devices. Medical supplies and
consumables are available for use.

Verbatim OE text:

Guidebook interpretation:
The written guidance shall address aspects related to the formulary, procurement, storage,

management shall cover all patient care areas across the organisation. All required

     dispensing, administration and monitoring the use of medications.
  2. A qualified individual shall supervise all pharmacy service activities.
  3. Medication management shall cover all patient care areas across the organisation.
  4. All required procedures under this chapter shall be documented as a
     'Medication Management Manual' — this is the specific container the Guidebook names.

Illustrative only:

    are mandatory (shall).

Documentation: OE is starred — written guidance implementing pharmacy services and
medication management must be on file. The Medication Management Manual is the named
documentary requirement.

MOM.2.a
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
A list of medications appropriate for the patients and as per the scope of the
organisation's clinical services is developed collaboratively by the multi-disciplinary
committee.

Guidebook interpretation:
The multidisciplinary committee shall prepare the organisation's formulary. The formulary
shall include medications necessary to meet the organisation's mission, patient needs and
scope of services. The formulary could be prepared keeping in view the "National List of
Essential Medicines" and "WHO Model List of Essential Medicines". It could also take into
consideration the harm potential of the medication, interactions with other medications
and likelihood of patient safety incidents. The organisation could look at the possibility
of having system-wise/speciality wise formulary. At a minimum, the formulary shall include
the name of the molecule, formulation and strength(s). The organisation shall endeavour
to limit the number of drug concentrations of a particular drug in the formulary. Implants
and devices are included in drugs and shall be included in the formulary.

Stop-work check: CORE — non-compliance is accreditation-blocking. MOM.2 has no
designated stop-work authority section. CORE status means a formulary must exist and be
committee-prepared; it does not create a stop-work trigger clause in this policy.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Committee shall prepare the formulary — collaborative preparation is mandatory.
  2. Formulary shall include medications necessary to meet the organisation's mission,
     patient needs and scope of services.
  3. At minimum, formulary entries shall include: name of the molecule, formulation,
     and strength(s) — this is the explicit minimum; preserve verbatim.
  4. Implants and devices are included in drugs and shall be included in the formulary —
     mandatory scope extension; do not omit.
  5. The organisation shall endeavour to limit the number of drug concentrations —
     "shall endeavour" is aspirational-mandatory (directional duty, not a hard cap).

Illustrative only:
  — NLEM and WHO Model List are examples ("could be prepared keeping in view") — not
    mandatory adoption of either specific list.
  — Harm potential, drug interactions, patient safety incidents "could also take into
    consideration" — evaluative criteria the organisation may apply; not a mandatory
    scoring framework.
  — System-wise/specialty-wise formulary "could look at the possibility" — optional.

CORE status: A formulary that does not exist, or exists but was not prepared by the
multidisciplinary committee, is a CORE gap.

MOM.3.a
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Medications are stored in a clean, safe and secure environment while incorporating the
manufacturer's recommendation(s).

Guidebook interpretation:
The medication storage space shall be clean, safe and secure. The organisation shall
adhere to the storage requirements of drugs as specified by the manufacturer. In the
absence of manufacturer's instructions, the organisation shall develop and implement
storage requirements. Storage requirements shall apply to all areas where medications are
stored, including clinical areas. Medications shall be protected from loss or theft
throughout the organisation. Some of the ways of ensuring this is to limit access to
medication storage areas to authorised team members, locking medication carts and never
leaving them unattended, or storing medications in an area that is continuously staffed.
It is preferable that the medication storage area is organised. Overall cleanliness of the
storage area shall be maintained. Vaccines shall be stored at the required temperature as
per manufacturers recommendations. Where appropriate, temperature monitoring of the room,
the cold storage area/refrigerator shall be done at least once a day. In case of areas
which are not open all days, it shall be done on all working days. Beyond expiry date drugs
(before disposal), shall be stored separately and away from drugs which are intended for
patient use.

Stop-work check: CORE — non-compliance is accreditation-blocking. MOM.3 stop-work
applies specifically to: LASA/concentration pairs stored together; emergency medications
missing/unreplenished; storage outside manufacturer recommendations. An immediate storage
condition that violates manufacturer requirements (e.g., unrefrigerated cold-chain
product) is a stop-work trigger at the storage location level.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Storage space shall be clean, safe and secure.
  2. Manufacturer storage requirements shall be adhered to throughout — applies to all
     drugs in all areas.
  3. In absence of manufacturer's instructions, the organisation shall develop and
     implement its own storage requirements.
  4. Storage requirements shall apply to all areas where medications are stored,
     including clinical areas — mandatory scope breadth.
  5. Medications shall be protected from loss or theft throughout the organisation.
  6. Overall cleanliness shall be maintained.
  7. Vaccines shall be stored at required temperature per manufacturer.
  8. Temperature monitoring of cold storage/refrigerator: at least once a day (or on all
     working days for areas not open daily).
  9. Beyond-expiry drugs shall be stored separately and away from drugs intended for
     patient use — mandatory physical separation.

Illustrative only:
  — Specific ways to prevent loss/theft (limit access, lock carts, continuously staffed
    area) are examples ("some of the ways").
  — "It is preferable that the medication storage area is organised" — organised storage
    is best practice, not a standalone mandatory standard.

CORE status: A clinical area storing drugs without applying manufacturer requirements
(e.g., unrefrigerated insulin in a ward) is a CORE gap and a stop-work trigger.

MOM.3.e
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
High-risk medications including look-alike, sound-alike medications and different
concentrations of the same medication are stored physically apart from each other.

Guidebook interpretation:
Many drugs in ampoules, vials or tablets may look-alike or sound-alike. These are
identified periodically, and the Look-alike Sound-alike medications (LASA) list shall be
made available in all units where drugs are stored. Different concentrations of the same
drug need to be identified. The list shall be developed from the hospital formulary. The
list will have to be revised at regular intervals depending on the changes in the formulary
and changes in the packaging (in case of look-alike). A good practice is to store the two
identified look-alike/sound-alike drugs and/or different concentrations of the same drugs
as far apart physically as is possible. This is in addition to regular storage practices.
In addition to the pharmacy, these storage practices shall be followed in patient care areas.

Stop-work check: CORE and starred. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: Finding LASA pairs or different
concentrations of the same drug stored physically together is an immediate stop-work
condition for that storage location. The stop-work authority in section 6 of this policy
applies. Issue from a non-compliant storage location is halted; immediate life-saving use
of the only available dose continues with escalation documented.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. LASA medications shall be identified periodically.
  2. LASA list shall be made available in all units where drugs are stored —
     mandatory availability at every storage location, not only pharmacy.
  3. Different concentrations of the same drug shall be identified.
  4. List shall be developed from the hospital formulary.
  5. List shall be revised at regular intervals (formulary changes, packaging changes).
  6. LASA storage practices shall be followed in patient care areas in addition to pharmacy.

MOM.6.d
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Dispensed medications are labelled.

Guidebook interpretation:
At a minimum, the label shall include the dosage instruction in a manner that the patient

GUIDEBOOK INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY FLAG (for policy writer):
The Guidebook first states "Labelling is applicable only for out-patients" and then
immediately states "This shall be applicable for both in-patients and out-patients."
The later statement is the correct, expanded scope and should be treated as the
operative requirement. The earlier statement is likely an OCR artefact or an obsolete
draft phrase that was not fully removed. The policy must reflect the broader scope:
in-patients and out-patients, and reconstituted drugs.

  3. Labelling shall be applicable for both in-patients and out-patients.
