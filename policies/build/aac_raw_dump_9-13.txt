AAC.9 — There is an established quality assurance and safety programme
         for imaging services.

Standard text (verbatim):
There is an established quality assurance and safety programme for imaging services.

Standard is NOT starred.

OE count: 11 | COREs: none | Stars: a★, e★, f★, i★ | Achievement: b, c | Excellence: d
STOP-WORK: YES — incomplete required safety/risk screening before radiation or MRI exposure;
                  failed or absent required radiation-safety or monitoring device for an
                  examination that would rely on it.
                  Pregnancy hold-for-review is hospital safety practice, not an NABH OE requirement.

AERB NOTE: The quality assurance programme for imaging services must meet statutory AERB
requirements. The Atomic Energy (Radiation Protection) Rules, 2004, govern radiation
protection, monitoring devices and related imaging-safety duties. This standard and AAC.8
both cross-reference AERB but from different angles: AAC.8 owns the AERB clearance and
PC-PNDT registration as service legal compliance; AAC.9 owns the ongoing QA tests, ALARA
protocols, screening, device testing and safety training.

BOUNDARY NOTE:
  — AAC.8 owns AERB clearance/PC-PNDT registration (legal compliance), TAT and critical
    intimation for imaging.
  — AAC.9 (this standard) owns the QA programme, ALARA, screening, device use/testing,
    training and signage.
  — PRE.4.a is cross-referenced in the Guidebook for consent before contrast, moderate–deep
    sedation, interventional procedures, and higher-risk findings on screening. Must not
    reproduce as code citation in policy.

AAC.9.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The quality assurance programme for imaging services is implemented.

Guidebook interpretation:
The quality assurance programme for imaging services is implemented. It is comprehensive —
addressing equipment, protocols, surveillance and safety — and meets statutory AERB
requirements. The programme includes tests for imaging equipment. Examples include
congruence of optical and radiation field, focal spot size, output consistency, leakage
rate, magnetic field homogeneity, slice position accuracy, phantom checks, and other tests
as applicable to the modalities present. Quality assurance for nuclear medicine (PET/gamma
camera/SPECT CT) and radiotherapy (linear accelerator, brachytherapy) is implemented as
per AERB guidelines where those services exist. The programme includes review of imaging
protocols to ensure optimum image quality with minimum possible dosage. Protocols follow
professional-body/academic guidance and, where relevant, the clinical diagnosis.

Stop-work check: Commitment and starred — no standalone stop-work at this OE for QA
schedule gaps. The stop-work for AAC.9 applies to screening failures (AAC.9.g) and device
failures (AAC.9.i). A failure to run the QA programme is a starred Commitment gap requiring
documented CAPA but is not itself an immediate stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. A comprehensive quality assurance programme for imaging services shall be implemented.
  2. The programme shall address equipment, protocols, surveillance and safety.
  3. The programme shall meet statutory AERB requirements.
  4. Equipment tests shall be performed as applicable to the modalities present (field
     congruence, output consistency, phantom checks, etc.).
  5. Nuclear medicine and radiotherapy QA shall be implemented per AERB guidelines where
     those services exist.
  6. Protocol review shall be included to ensure optimum image quality with minimum
     possible dosage.

Illustrative only:
  — The equipment-test examples (congruence, focal spot, output, leakage, homogeneity,
    slice position, phantom checks) are illustrative; the list is not exhaustive and
    applies to modalities the hospital operates.

Documentation: OE is starred — QA programme document, equipment test records, protocol
review file, and nuclear medicine/radiotherapy QA records where applicable must be on file.

AAC.9.b
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
A system is in place to ensure the appropriateness of the investigations and procedures
for the clinical indication.

Guidebook interpretation:
A system is in place to ensure the appropriateness of investigations and procedures for
the clinical indication. Investigation orders are screened before imaging or interventional
procedures to ensure they are appropriate — in terms of current best-practice guidelines
and patient safety — for the clinical indication. If a request is not appropriate,
alternate investigations are offered in consultation with the treating doctor. For example,
if breast abscess is suspected as the cause of fever and breast lump in a lactating
25-year-old woman, mammography is inappropriate; ultrasound of the breast is appropriate.
The organisation maintains a record of instances where the requested test was modified
after consultation and may audit effectiveness; findings may be discussed in clinico-
radiological meetings.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. An
inappropriateness system gap is an Achievement accreditation gap and a CAPA item, not an
immediate stop-work trigger. (The AAC.9 stop-work applies to screening and device failure,
not to appropriateness decisions.)

Mandatory (shall, Achievement level):
  1. A system shall be in place to screen investigation orders before imaging or
     interventional procedures for appropriateness to the clinical indication.
  2. If a request is not appropriate, alternate investigations shall be offered in
     consultation with the treating doctor.

Illustrative only:
  — The mammography vs ultrasound example is an illustrative case, not an exhaustive rule.
  — "The organisation maintains a record... and may audit effectiveness" — maintaining the
    record is the mandatory expectation; effectiveness audit via clinico-radiological
    meetings is aspirational best practice.

Achievement status: Evidenced by an appropriateness screening method or checklist, a log
of modified investigations, and records of consultation with treating doctors.

Documentation: OE is not starred — appropriateness screening method, modification log,
and audit notes discussed in clinico-radiological meetings are the expected evidence.

AAC.9.c
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The programme addresses periodic internal / external peer review of imaging results using
appropriate sampling.

Guidebook interpretation:
The programme addresses periodic internal or external peer review of imaging results using
appropriate sampling. A peer review system reviews imaging results of CT and MRI in a
structured manner. Sample size and periodicity for each modality are defined by the
organisation; at a minimum, one percent. Peer review may be performed by the head of
department or a group of peers, with blinding of original reports. Discrepancies are
graded on severity and impact on patient management; CAPA to minimise recurrence is
documented. RADPEER is one such scoring scale. The purpose is prevention of future errors
and continuous quality improvement, not computation of individual error rates. Results may
be discussed in discrepancy meetings and documented.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. Failure to
conduct peer review is an Achievement accreditation gap, not an immediate stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall, Achievement level):
  1. A peer review system shall review imaging results of CT and MRI in a structured manner.
  2. Sample size and periodicity shall be defined by the organisation; at a minimum, one
     percent.
  3. Discrepancies shall be graded on severity and impact on patient management.
  4. CAPA to minimise recurrence shall be documented.

Illustrative only:
  — RADPEER is a named scoring example; the organisation may use any recognised grading scale.
  — Blinding of original reports, discrepancy meetings — aspirational methodology; the
    mandatory requirement is structured review with graded discrepancies and CAPA.

Achievement status: Evidenced by defined sample size (minimum one percent), blinded peer-
review records with discrepancy grading, discrepancy meeting notes and related CAPA.

Documentation: OE is not starred — peer-review records with grading and CAPA, and
discrepancy meeting notes where held, are the expected evidence.

AAC.9.f
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The radiation-safety programme is implemented.

Guidebook interpretation:
The radiation-safety programme is implemented. It follows guidelines laid down by AERB
and implements the As Low As Reasonably Achievable (ALARA) principle in investigations
involving radiation, including screening of patients at high risk for radiation. Routine
unjustified radiation is avoided — for example routine daily chest radiograph for all ICU
patients cannot be justified, more so in paediatric or neonatal ICUs. CT protocols are
modified to use the lowest exposure parameters that maintain image quality appropriate for
the clinical indication — for example low-dose CT for ureteric calculi versus higher dose
for renal tumour. Appropriate safety precautions per AERB guidelines for nuclear medicine
and radiation oncology are followed where those services exist. This programme is aligned
with the organisation's safety programme.

Stop-work check: Commitment and starred — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE
specifically. The AAC.9 stop-work for missing screening (AAC.9.g) and failed devices
(AAC.9.i) is where the immediate stop gates sit. A failure to implement the radiation-
safety programme overall is a starred Commitment gap; individual radiation violations
that continue after identification are escalated through the stop-work and CAPA path.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. The radiation-safety programme shall follow AERB guidelines.
  2. The ALARA principle shall be implemented in all investigations involving radiation.
  3. Routine unjustified radiation shall be avoided (ICU daily chest radiograph example;
     paediatric and neonatal ICU especially).
  4. CT protocols shall use the lowest exposure parameters that maintain adequate image
     quality for the clinical indication.
  5. AERB precautions for nuclear medicine and radiation oncology shall be followed where
     those services exist.
  6. The programme shall be aligned with the organisation's safety programme.

Illustrative only:
  — The ICU chest radiograph and low-dose CT examples are illustrative of the ALARA
    principle; the rule extends to all modalities and clinical contexts.

Documentation: OE is starred — radiation-safety programme document aligned with AERB and
organisation safety, ALARA protocol examples and unjustified-exposure avoidance records,
and nuclear medicine/radiation-oncology AERB precaution records (where applicable) must
be on file.

AAC.9.g
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Patients are appropriately screened for safety / risk before imaging.

Guidebook interpretation:
Patients are appropriately screened for safety/risk before imaging. Patients in the
child-bearing age group who need radiation exposure are screened for pregnancy. Patients
undergoing MRI are screened for any magnetic substance. Screening is documented. Screening
also applies to attendants accompanying the patient or child into the imaging area.
Informed consent is taken for contrast injection, moderate–deep sedation, interventional
procedures, and whenever higher risk due to imaging is found on risk screening. Refer to
PRE.4.a.

[Note: "Refer to PRE.4.a" is a cross-reference to another OE code — must not appear in
policy text. The informed-consent method for these situations is owned by the PRE chapter.]

Stop-work check: Commitment — STOP-WORK TRIGGER: do not proceed with radiation or MRI
exposure when required safety/risk screening has not been completed and documented for the
patient (and for any attendant entering the imaging area). As hospital safety practice
(not an NABH OE requirement), do not proceed when pregnancy status is positive or
uncertain for a radiation examination until the «radiologist or imaging in-charge» has
reviewed and documented the decision. Do not proceed with MRI when magnetic-substance
screening is incomplete or positive for a contraindication.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Patients in the child-bearing age group requiring radiation exposure shall be screened
     for pregnancy before the examination.
  2. Patients undergoing MRI shall be screened for any magnetic substance before the
     examination.
  3. Screening shall be documented.
  4. Screening also applies to attendants accompanying a patient or child into the imaging
     area — they shall be screened before entry.
  5. Informed consent shall be taken for contrast injection, moderate–deep sedation,
     interventional procedures, and higher-risk findings on risk screening (consent method
     per PRE).

Illustrative only:
  — The pregnancy-hold safety practice (no-proceed until reviewed) is hospital safety
    governance; it is not an NABH OE-level mandate but is strongly recommended as a stop-
    work control.

Documentation: OE is not starred — documented pregnancy screening, MRI magnetic-substance
screening, and consent records for contrast/sedation/interventional procedures are the
expected evidence.

AAC.9.i
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Radiation-safety and monitoring devices are periodically tested, and results are documented.

Guidebook interpretation:
Radiation-safety and monitoring devices are periodically tested, and results are
documented. Protective devices such as lead aprons are exposed to X-ray, fluoroscopy or
CT scout view to verify cracks and damage. Monitoring is done periodically as per national
guidelines. Images of the checks are stored (physical or electronic). Corrective and/or
preventive action is taken and documented where appropriate. TLD badges are replaced by
fresh badges supplied by accredited laboratories according to the frequency recommended
by AERB.

Stop-work check: Commitment and starred — STOP-WORK TRIGGER: do not proceed with a
radiation examination that would rely on a radiation-safety or monitoring device that has
failed its periodic test (for example a cracked lead apron). A failed device is removed
from use until repaired or replaced; the stop-work applies to examinations that would have
relied on that specific device.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Radiation-safety and monitoring devices shall be periodically tested.
  2. Test results shall be documented.
  3. Protective devices (lead aprons) shall be exposed to X-ray, fluoroscopy or CT scout
     view to verify cracks and damage.
  4. Monitoring shall be done periodically as per national guidelines.
  5. Images of the checks shall be stored (physical or electronic).
  6. CAPA shall be taken and documented where a device test result warrants it.
  7. TLD badges shall be replaced per AERB-recommended frequency by fresh badges from
     accredited laboratories.

Illustrative only:
  (none — all elements above are "shall")

Documentation: OE is starred — periodic lead-apron/shield test images and results, TLD
replacement records per AERB-recommended frequency, and CAPA/removal-from-use records
for failed devices must be on file.

AAC.10.a
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
During all phases of care, there is a qualified individual identified as responsible for
the patient's care.

Guidebook interpretation:
During all phases of care at the organisation, a qualified individual is identified as
responsible for the patient's care. That individual is a qualified doctor. A team may
deliver care; the hospital record still names one doctor as responsible. The responsible
doctor's name is recorded in the patient record at first contact and updated when
responsibility changes. When the named doctor is off duty, the covering doctor is identified
and the change is recorded. Out-patient, emergency, in-patient, intensive care and day-
care each keep the same rule: no patient is without a named responsible doctor.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. A patient
without a named responsible doctor in any care setting is a Commitment gap and a safety
concern addressed through the non-negotiable rule and CAPA, not an immediate stop-work
gate.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. A qualified individual (doctor) shall be identified as responsible for the patient's
     care during all phases of care.
  2. The responsible doctor's name shall be recorded in the patient record.
  3. When the named doctor is off duty, the covering doctor shall be identified and
     the change recorded.

Illustrative only:
  (none — all elements above are "shall")

Documentation: OE is not starred — patient record entry naming the responsible doctor
for each phase of care, covering-doctor record when the named doctor is off duty, and
quarterly audit sample of responsible-doctor documentation across care settings are the
expected evidence.

Patient care is coordinated in all care settings within the organisation.

Guidebook interpretation:
Patient care is coordinated among care providers in each care setting within the
organisation: out-patient, emergency, in-patient, intensive care and any other setting

the providers working in that setting so that orders, observations and pending tasks are
not lost when the patient moves from one setting to another. The treating doctor and the
nurse in charge of the area ensure the receiving setting knows the clinical status and
the next required actions before the patient arrives or as soon as care begins there.

setting coordination are a Commitment gap and CAPA item, not an immediate stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Patient care shall be coordinated among care providers in each care setting — OPD,
     emergency, in-patient, ICU and any other setting the organisation uses.
  2. The patient's requirements shall be communicated to providers in the receiving setting
     so that orders, observations and pending tasks are not lost.

Illustrative only:
  (none — both elements above are "shall")

Documentation: OE is not starred — communication or transfer notes when patients move
across care settings, and quarterly audit sample of cross-setting coordination, are the
expected evidence.

Do not proceed with radiation or MRI exposure when required safety/risk screening has not
been completed and documented for the patient (and for any attendant entering the imaging
area).

As hospital safety practice (not an NABH objective-element requirement), do not proceed
when pregnancy status is positive or uncertain for a radiation examination until the
«radiologist or imaging in-charge» has reviewed and documented the decision. Do not
proceed with MRI when magnetic-substance screening is incomplete or positive for a
contraindication.

Do not proceed when a required radiation-safety or monitoring device has failed its
periodic test (for example cracked lead apron) or when required TLD badges are not
available for staff working with radiation sources.

Stop-work applies to the imaging exposure or MRI examination, not to emergency clinical
stabilisation outside the imaging room.

The person who stops tells the «Imaging In-Charge» or the «Radiation Safety Officer»
the same shift. Refusing to expose without screening or with failed safety devices is
not a disciplinary matter.
