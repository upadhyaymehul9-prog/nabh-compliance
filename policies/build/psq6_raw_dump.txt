Standard text (verbatim):
The patient safety and quality improvement programme are supported by the
management.

OE count: 6 | COREs: none | Stars: none

PSQ.6.a
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The management creates a culture of safety.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF pp. 229–230):
The management needs to ensure the adoption of behaviours that promote patient
safety. Some of the key features required for a culture of safety are sharing
information, reporting occurrences of incidents, learning from safety incident
analysis, blame-free culture and encouragement of collaboration across
disciplines and departments. The key components of patient safety culture are
informed culture, reporting culture, learning culture, just culture and flexible
culture.

The management needs to measure its safety culture regularly (at least once a
year). This shall be measured using validated surveys. For example, the
Manchester Patient Safety Framework (MaPSaF), Safety Attitudes Questionnaire,
AHRQ Surveys on Patient Safety Culture (SOPS™). The management shall act on
their patient safety culture assessment results.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no stop-work trigger. Two mandatory (shall)
actions: measure safety culture at least once a year using validated surveys,
and act on the assessment results. The survey instruments (MaPSaF, SAQ, AHRQ
SOPS) are illustrative examples — the organisation selects a validated survey
appropriate to it. The five culture components (informed, reporting, learning,
just, flexible) and the five safety-culture features (sharing information,
reporting incidents, learning from analysis, blame-free culture, collaboration)
describe the target state — they are not a mandatory enumerated checklist.

PSQ.6.b
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The leaders at all levels in the organisation are aware of the intent of the
patient safety and quality improvement programme and the approach to its
implementation.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 229):
The organisation and departmental leaders are aware of the patient safety and
quality improvement programme, its intent and applicability to the respective
areas and how it contributes to the organisation as a whole.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. Awareness is required; no
specific mechanism for ensuring awareness is mandated. Any draft describing
mandatory awareness briefings, signed acknowledgements or documented
orientation must treat these as examples of how awareness may be achieved, not
as source-prescribed requirements.

PSQ.6.c
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Departmental leaders are involved in patient safety and quality improvement.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 229):
To effectively implement this, each department could have its department
objectives/key performance indicators including clinical indicators, and the
responsibility of achieving them could be that of the leader/designated person.
This could be discussed during departmental meeting.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. All three mechanisms
(department KPIs, leader/designated-person responsibility, departmental-meeting
discussion) are illustrative (could) — none is mandated. The requirement is
that departmental leaders are involved; the form of that involvement is for the
organisation to determine.

PSQ.6.d
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Organisation earmarks adequate funds from its annual budget in this regard.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 230):
Appropriate fund allocation is done by the organisation for the smooth
functioning of the patient safety and quality improvement programme. The budget
could be earmarked based on previous year's spending. If no data is available,
the organisation could make a beginning by earmarking a budget but reviewing it
at the end of six months to make any necessary modifications. The management
makes available adequate resources required for patient safety and quality
improvement programme. Resources shall include men, material, machine, money,
milieu, measurement and method. These shall be in steady supply to ensure that
the programme functions smoothly.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger. Two mandatory (shall)
elements: resources shall include the seven categories (men, material, machine,
money, milieu, measurement and method), and these shall be in steady supply.
The previous-year-spending basis and the six-month-review mechanism are
advisory (could). The seven-M resource list is verbatim in the source and must
be preserved — it is not an invention.

PSQ.6.e
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The management identifies organisational performance improvement targets.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 230):
The management shall identify the organisation and department level quality
objectives, set targets, monitor them (at least once in three months) and
modify the target (at least annually). The targets shall be shared with the
faculty and staff and regular feedback taken.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no stop-work trigger. Four mandatory (shall)
actions: identify objectives, set targets, monitor them (at least once in three
months), and modify them (at least annually). Sharing with faculty and staff is
also mandatory (shall). Two exact frequencies — "at least once in three months"
and "at least annually" — are verbatim in the source and must be preserved
without softening.

PSQ.6.f
Level: Excellence | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The management uses the feedback obtained from the work force to improve
patient safety and quality improvement programme.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 230):
The feedback shall be obtained from the staff on their understanding and use of
the safety and quality systems. Feedback may be obtained once a year through
staff surveys, but it is also important that staff workforce feel able to raise
concerns whenever they occur. These inputs shall be used to improve patient
safety and quality improvement programmes.

Stop-work check: Excellence — no stop-work trigger. Two mandatory (shall)
elements: obtain feedback from staff on their understanding and use of safety
and quality systems, and use those inputs to improve the programmes. The annual
staff survey is described with "may be obtained" — it is a recommended
frequency/mechanism, not a mandatory prescription. Enabling staff to raise
concerns at any time is described as important but not with shall — it is an
additional good-practice pointer.
