CHAPTER INTENT (official Standards PDF, verbatim):
The organisation implements an effective healthcare associated infection prevention and
control programme. The programme is documented and aims at reducing / eliminating infection
risks to patients, visitors and providers of care. The programme is implemented across the
organisation, including clinical areas and support services. The organisation provides proper
facilities and adequate resources to support the infection prevention and control programme.
The organisation measures and acts to prevent or reduce the risk of healthcare associated
infection in patients and staff. The organisation has an effective antimicrobial management
programme through regularly updated antimicrobial policy based on local data and monitors
its implementation. Programme also includes monitoring of antimicrobials usage in the
organisation. Surveillance activities are incorporated in the infection prevention and
control programme. The programme includes disinfection / sterilisation activities and
biomedical waste (BMW) management.

IPC.1.a
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The infection prevention and control programme is documented, which aims at preventing and
reducing the risk of healthcare associated infections in the hospital.

Guidebook interpretation:
The written guidance shall be directed at prevention and control of infection in all areas
of the hospital and include its monitoring. The organisation shall have infection prevention
and control manual (IPC manual) that shall incorporate the structure of the programme,
overall aims and objectives, all processes, activities and surveillance procedures related
to the programme. This shall be based on organisational priorities, current scientific
knowledge, guidelines from international/national and professional bodies and statutory
requirements, wherever applicable. The hospital implements strategies, education, and
evidence-based activities to reduce infection risks. The periodic assessment of risk and
the setting of risk reduction goals guide.

Stop-work check: CORE and starred. Non-compliance is accreditation-blocking. IPC.1 is not
in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS; there is no stop-work authority section in this policy. However,
CORE status means the written IPC programme as a documented manual must exist — a programme
that exists only in meeting minutes is not compliant. Absence of the manual is a CORE gap
identified at assessment.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Written guidance shall be directed at prevention and control of infection in all areas
     of the hospital and include its monitoring.
  2. The organisation shall have an IPC manual incorporating: the structure of the
     programme; overall aims and objectives; all processes, activities and surveillance
     procedures related to the programme.
  3. The manual shall be based on organisational priorities, current scientific knowledge,
     guidelines from international/national and professional bodies, and statutory
     requirements where applicable.

Illustrative only:
  — "Implements strategies, education, and evidence-based activities to reduce infection
    risks" — describes what the programme does; not a separate standalone checklist item.
  — "Periodic assessment of risk and the setting of risk reduction goals guide" — this
    sentence is the basis for IPC.1.d (validated assessment tool); it is illustrative
    context here.

Documentation: OE is starred — the IPC Manual as a standing document must be on file.
A collection of meeting minutes without a consolidated manual is not this asterisked element.

CORE status: A programme that is undocumented or exists only in meeting minutes is a
CORE gap.

IPC.3.a
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation adheres to standard precautions at all times.

Guidebook interpretation:
Adherence to standard precautions is one of the fundamental tenets of infection prevention
and control. In every area of the organisation, standard precautions shall be adhered.
Refer to the glossary for "standard precautions". The components of standard precautions
include hand hygiene, appropriate use of personal protective equipment, respiratory
etiquettes, safe injection practices, sterile instruments and devices, clean and
disinfected environmental surfaces, cleaning and disinfection of equipment, and needle-
stick and sharps injury prevention. Appropriate preparation of body parts before a
procedure and the use of disinfected/sterilised instruments is ensured. The organisation
adheres to hand-hygiene guidelines.

[Note: The last sentence is the transitional OE header for IPC.3.b — included in the
OCR run-on. It is not part of IPC.3.a's interpretation. Discarded for this OE.]

Stop-work check: CORE and starred. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: Starting a clinical procedure when
standard precautions are not in place (PPE, sharps safety, respiratory etiquette as the
situation requires) is a stop-work trigger. The stop-work authority in Section 6 of this
policy applies.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Standard precautions shall be adhered in every area of the organisation, at all times
     — the scope is universal and unconditional.
  2. Appropriate preparation of body parts before a procedure shall be ensured.

IPC.6.f
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Surveillance includes monitoring the effectiveness of housekeeping services.

Guidebook interpretation:
Monitoring of the effectiveness of housekeeping services shall be done regularly. The
organisation shall define the periodicity. This is applicable even if the housekeeping
services are outsourced. It is mandatory to capture the effectiveness of the housekeeping
activities and not just verify if the housekeeping activity has been done as per the
defined frequency. To capture effectiveness, the organisation shall identify desired
outcome parameters for housekeeping activities.

IMPORTANT NEGATIVE REQUIREMENTS FROM GUIDEBOOK (mandatory prohibitions):
  — "This need not mean routine environmental sampling." — routine microbiological swabs
    and culture plates shall NOT be collected routinely from OT and other high-risk areas
    for monitoring clean environment.
  — "Periodic fumigation is not recommended."
  — "Fogging is recommended only in following conditions: Commissioning of new OT;
    Modification/renovation of OT; Significant increase in SSI with evidence of
    evolvement of environment." — fogging is not a routine housekeeping-monitoring
    activity.

Stop-work check: CORE — non-compliance is accreditation-blocking. IPC.6 is not in
STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. Monitoring housekeeping frequency compliance only (without capturing
effectiveness outcomes) is a CORE gap.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Effectiveness monitoring of housekeeping services shall be done regularly.
  2. Periodicity shall be defined by the organisation.
  3. Applicable even when outsourced.
  4. Effectiveness, not only frequency compliance, shall be captured.
  5. Desired outcome parameters for housekeeping shall be identified.
  6. Routine microbiological environmental sampling shall NOT be used as the routine
     monitoring method.
  7. Periodic fumigation shall NOT be performed.
  8. Fogging shall be performed ONLY in the three specified conditions (new OT
     commissioning; OT modification/renovation; significant SSI increase with environmental
     evidence) — not routinely.

Illustrative only:
  — Cleanliness of hospital premises, patient feedback, staff feedback: examples of
    outcome parameters ("For example") — not a mandatory closed list.
  — Data captured using a checklist: suggested approach.

CORE status: Monitoring only whether housekeeping was done on schedule, without evidence
of outcome effectiveness, is a CORE gap.

STOP-WORK — IPC.3 (Standard 3: Infection Prevention and Control in Clinical Areas)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do not start a clinical procedure when standard precautions are not in place (PPE, sharps
safety, cough etiquette as the situation requires), when hand hygiene has not been done
at the required moment, or when injection/infusion practice would reuse a needle or
syringe.

Do not start a restricted antimicrobial without the organisation's antimicrobial-
stewardship path (documented indication and, where required, approval).

Stop-work applies to the procedure or first dose start. Immediate life-saving care
continues with the best available precautions and is documented.

The person who stops tells the [treating doctor] and the [Infection Prevention and Control
Officer] the same shift. Refusing an unsafe start is not a disciplinary matter.

STOP-WORK — IPC.4 (Standard 4: Infection Prevention and Control in Support Services)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do not handle biomedical waste without the required colour-coded segregation and PPE.

Do not start construction or renovation in a patient-care area until the infection-risk
plan for that work has been completed and the [Infection Prevention and Control Officer]
has agreed the controls.

Stop-work applies to the waste-handling act and to the construction/renovation start.
Emergency repair that cannot wait follows the documented emergency-works IPC controls and
is recorded.

The person who stops tells the [Infection Prevention and Control Officer] and the
[Medical Superintendent] the same shift. Refusing unsafe waste handling or unplanned works
is not a disciplinary matter.

STOP-WORK — IPC.5 (Standard 5: Prevention of Healthcare Associated Infections in Patients)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do not insert an indwelling urinary catheter, insert a central line, start invasive
ventilation, or make a surgical incision unless the organisation's prevention bundle for
that HAI (CAUTI, CLABSI, VAP or SSI) is in place for this patient.

Stop-work applies to the device insertion or incision. Life-saving airway or haemorrhage

The person who stops tells the [treating doctor] and the [Infection Prevention and Control
Officer] the same shift. Refusing an insertion without the bundle is not a disciplinary
matter.
