══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

COP.5.a
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Cardio-pulmonary resuscitation services are available and provided to patients
at all times.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 109):
The organisation shall document the procedure for cardio-pulmonary
resuscitation (CPR) for adults across all areas in the organisation. This shall
be in consonance with accepted practices. Where appropriate, it shall also
address obstetric, paediatric and neonatal patients. The organisation shall
ensure that medical equipment for resuscitation and medications for basic and
advanced life support are provided in standardised manner. Basic life support
shall be initiated as soon as a condition requiring CPR is identified. This shall
be implemented in all areas of the organisation. The protocols could be
displayed prominently in all critical areas such as emergency, ICU, OT and
all crash carts etc.

Stop-work check: YES — COP.5 is in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. The stop-work
trigger arises from the "at all times" and "all areas" requirements. Not having
CPR-capable personnel or equipment in a patient-care area at the moment of
need is the triggering condition.

Mandatory (shall):
  — The CPR procedure for adults shall be documented across all areas.
  — Documentation shall be in consonance with accepted practices.
  — Medical equipment for resuscitation and medications for BLS and ALS shall
    be provided in a standardised manner (shall).
  — Basic life support shall be initiated as soon as a condition requiring CPR
    is identified (shall — time-critical trigger, no delay permitted).
  — Implemented in all areas of the organisation (shall — no area exception).

Non-mandatory (illustrative/optional):
  — Obstetric, paediatric and neonatal CPR addressed "where appropriate" — the
    scope modifier is conditional on the organisation's services.
  — Displaying protocols prominently in critical areas "could" be done —
    advisory, not mandatory placement requirement.
  — Specific critical areas listed (ICU, OT, crash carts) are examples, not an
    exhaustive mandatory list.

Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:

COP.5.d
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The events during cardio-pulmonary resuscitation are recorded.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 110):
In the actual event of cardio-pulmonary resuscitation, or a mock drill of the
same, all the activities along with the personnel attended shall be recorded.
At the minimum, it will include timeliness of response, availability of human
resources, equipment, drugs, and barriers if any. The recording could be done
using the pre-defined procedural checklist and by monitoring whether the
prescribed activity has been performed properly and in the right sequence.

It is a good practice to debrief team members regarding the necessary
immediate corrective and preventive action.

Stop-work check: YES — the recording requirement applies to both actual CPR
events and mock drills. Failure to record is a compliance gap but is not itself
a stop-work trigger during the event; the recording can follow. However, the
stop-work framework for COP.5 covers the absence of CPR-readiness, not the
recording gap.

Mandatory (shall / minimum):
  — All activities and personnel attending shall be recorded — both actual events
    and mock drills (shall).
  — At a minimum, the record shall include: timeliness of response, availability
    of human resources, equipment, drugs, and barriers if any (minimum content
    — preserve these five categories verbatim).

Non-mandatory:
  — The recording method "could" be a pre-defined procedural checklist — the
    form of recording is the organisation's choice; the content minimum is fixed.
  — Debriefing on immediate CAPA is "a good practice" — recommended but
    not a shall in this OE.

to the post-event review cycle.

COP.6.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Nursing care is provided to patients in accordance with written guidance.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 111–112):
The written guidance could be in the form of a nursing manual/SOPs
incorporating various basic nursing practices and procedures. Examples of these
are monitoring vital parameters, administration of medications, basic hygiene
needs of the patient, etc.

Care of patients in specific clinical situations shall be guided by nursing clinical
practice guidelines based on best clinical practices. The nursing clinical care
guidelines/pathways shall be reviewed annually at the minimum, and revised as
appropriate.

Examples of nursing clinical practice guidelines include prevention of fall,
prevention of development of pressure ulcers in an in-patient, and deep venous
thrombosis risk assessment and prevention.

Stop-work check: Commitment — COP.6 not in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. No
stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  — Nursing clinical practice guidelines based on best clinical practices shall
    guide care of patients in specific clinical situations (shall).
  — Nursing clinical care guidelines/pathways shall be reviewed annually at the
    minimum, and revised as appropriate (shall — exact minimum frequency:
    annually; "revised as appropriate" is conditional).

EXACT MANDATORY FREQUENCY — preserve verbatim:
  — "reviewed annually at the minimum" — do not soften to "periodically."

Non-mandatory (illustrative):
  — Written guidance "could" be a nursing manual/SOPs — the form is the
    organisation's choice.
  — Examples of basic nursing practices (vital parameters, medications, hygiene)
    are illustrative — not a mandatory checklist of what the guidance must cover.
  — Examples of clinical practice guidelines (fall prevention, pressure ulcer
    prevention, DVT risk assessment) are illustrative — not the only guidelines
    required; the organisation selects guidelines appropriate to its clinical scope.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the written guidance is the required
documentation.

COP.7 — Clinical Procedures Performed Safely
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Standard text (verbatim):
Clinical procedures are performed in a safe manner.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.7.a
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
Clinical procedures are performed based on the clinical needs of the patient.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 113):
The decision to perform a procedure shall be based on the clinical needs of the
patient, in consonance with standard treatment guidelines and/or sound clinical
practice for the given condition/procedure. A qualified medical practitioner
decides if the procedure is indicated. When multiple procedure options exist,
the decision shall be based on which option is likely to provide the best
outcome, and also taking into consideration, the patient wishes and safety.
The organisation could conduct a clinical audit of various procedures to achieve
the best possible outcomes.

Stop-work check: YES — COP.7 is in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. The stop-work
trigger for COP.7 centres on wrong-patient/wrong-procedure/wrong-site
verification (COP.7.d), not on COP.7.a's clinical indication decision.

Mandatory (shall):
  — Decision shall be based on clinical needs (shall).
  — Decision shall be in consonance with standard treatment guidelines and/or
    sound clinical practice (shall).
  — A qualified medical practitioner shall decide if the procedure is indicated
    (shall — qualification requirement is mandatory).
  — When multiple options exist, decision based on best outcome and patient
    wishes and safety (shall).

Non-mandatory:
  — Clinical audit of procedures "could" be conducted — advisory, not mandatory.

[Guidebook cross-ref: none for COP.7.a specifically.]

Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

The written guidance is a broad guideline applicable to all procedures —

COP.8.g
Level: Achievement | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation shall implement a quality assurance programme.*

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 119):
The written guidance for quality assurance could be developed individually, or
it could be a part of the organisation's overall quality-improvement programme.
The quality assurance programme shall involve all aspects of the functioning
in the blood centre.

The blood centre participates in External Quality Assurance Scheme (EQAS)/
Proficiency Testing Programme (PT) or a suitable interlaboratory comparison
when the same is not available.

Results of IQC/EQAS/PT/inter-lab testing are monitored and appropriate
corrective and preventive action taken for continual quality improvement and
records of the same maintained.

Appropriate sampling of blood/products and reagents shall be done at defined
frequency for internal quality control, as per guidelines.

The blood centre shall collect data on key performance indicators as part of its
quality improvement programme. An example of a process measure is wastage
of blood and blood products and an example of outcome measure is transfusion
reactions.

The collected data shall be collated, analysed and used for further improvements.
The improvements shall be monitored for sustenance.

Stop-work check: Achievement — no real-time stop-work trigger from the QA
programme itself.

Mandatory (shall):
  — QA programme shall involve all aspects of blood centre functioning (shall).
  — Blood centre participates in EQAS/PT or suitable interlaboratory comparison
    when EQAS/PT is not available (stated without "should" or "preferably" —
    participation is mandatory, with an alternative path when EQAS/PT is
    unavailable).
  — IQC/EQAS/PT/inter-lab results shall be monitored and CAPA taken (shall);
    records maintained (shall).
  — Appropriate sampling of blood/products and reagents shall be done at defined
    frequency for internal quality control, as per guidelines (shall — frequency
    is guideline-driven, not a specific number to invent).
  — KPI data shall be collected (shall); data shall be collated, analysed and used
    for improvements (shall); improvements monitored for sustenance (shall).

Non-mandatory:
  — QA guidance "could" be developed individually or as part of overall QI —
    the form is the organisation's choice.
  — Examples of KPIs (wastage, transfusion reactions) are illustrative — the
    organisation selects appropriate indicators for its scope.

Documentation: This OE is starred — records of IQC/EQAS/PT results, CAPA,
and KPI data are the required documentation.

Note: This is Achievement level. Present it as an advanced quality commitment
in the policy, not at the same weight as basic Commitment OEs.

DRAFTING NOTES — COP.8
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. STOP-WORK TRIGGER (COP.8): The primary trigger is releasing or administering
   blood without completing pre-transfusion verification steps (patient ID,
   blood group verification, compatibility label, label retention, TTI/serology
   before release). Secondary triggers: quarantined units into inventory before
   re-testing, mixing untested and tested units, failure to detect/respond to
   suspected adverse reactions.

2. NINE WRITTEN-GUIDANCE SCOPE ELEMENTS (COP.8.b) — preserve all nine verbatim;
   they are stated inclusions, not examples.

3. EIGHT TRANSFUSION WRITTEN-GUIDANCE ELEMENTS (COP.8.d) — preserve all eight;
   they are stated mandatory content requirements.

4. HBTC IS MANDATORY (COP.8.d): A Hospital Blood Transfusion Committee shall
   exist. Its composition has four named mandatory roles; chair being a clinician
   is advisory. HBTC meeting frequency is "periodically" — do not specify a
   number (none is given).

5. HAEMOVIGILANCE PROGRAMME OF INDIA (COP.8.f): Participation is a shall —
   not advisory. Do not weaken.

6. EQAS/PT PARTICIPATION (COP.8.g): Mandatory, with interlaboratory comparison
   as the alternative when EQAS/PT is unavailable.

7. TIME FRAMES (COP.8.e): Emergency and routine TATs are organisation-defined.
   Do not invent numerical time frames (minutes, hours) — the Guidebook
   prescribes none.

8. MISMATCH PREVENTION (COP.8.d): The five example measures are illustrative,
   not a mandatory checklist. The obligation is the prevention principle; the
   examples show how to achieve it.

9. ACHIEVEMENT OELEVELS (COP.8.f, g): Present these as advanced quality and
   haemovigilance commitments — at a higher weight than Commitment OEs but
   clearly not basic compliance floors.
