IPC.7 — Infection Prevention Measures include Sterilisation and/or Disinfection
         of Instruments, Equipment and Devices
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Standard text (verbatim):
Infection prevention measures include sterilisation and / or disinfection of
instruments, equipment and devices.

Standard is NOT starred.

OE count: 5 | COREs: b | Stars: b★, c★, d★, e★ | Achievement: none | Excellence: none
STOP-WORK: YES — issue an item from CSSD/sterile store when sterilisation validation
                   has failed or a recall is in effect.

Prepared by: CSSD In-Charge | Doc no: HCO/IPC/POL/07

IPC.7.a
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation provides adequate space and appropriate zoning for sterilisation
activities.

Guidebook interpretation:
Adequacy of space refers to the central sterile services department (CSSD), which
shall have a suitable location, proper layout (unidirectional flow, zoning) and
separation of clean and dirty areas. Sufficient space shall be available to ensure
that the activities can be performed properly. It is preferable to have separate
areas for receiving, washing, cleaning, packing, sterilisation, sterile storage and
issue. A good reference is Hospital Infection Society India (HISI) and WHO guidelines.

[Note: "HB cone." at end of Guidebook source is an OCR artefact. Discarded.]

Stop-work check: Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. The IPC.7
stop-work applies to item issue; layout deficiencies are CAPA items raised at assessment
and IPC rounds. A CSSD without a clean/dirty separation or unidirectional flow is an
audit gap, not an immediate stop-work trigger.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. CSSD (or the sterilisation area the organisation uses) shall have a suitable
     location.
  2. Layout shall have unidirectional flow and zoning.
  3. Separation of clean and dirty areas shall be maintained.
  4. Sufficient space shall be available to ensure sterilisation activities can be
     performed properly.

Illustrative only:
  — Separate areas for receiving, washing, cleaning, packing, sterilisation, sterile
    storage and issue: "It is preferable" — aspirational; the mandatory requirement is
    adequate space with clean/dirty separation and proper flow/zoning.
  — HISI and WHO guidelines: named references, informational.

Documentation: No star — IPC round/audit records confirming adequate space and correct
zoning are the required evidence.

IPC.7.b
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Cleaning, packing, disinfection and / or sterilisation, storing and the issue of
items is done as per the written guidance.

Guidebook interpretation:
The written guidance shall be in consonance with national and/or international guidelines.
A good reference is "CDC Guideline for Disinfection and Sterilization in Healthcare
Facilities, 2008". Other references include the Hospital Infection Society India (HISI)
guidelines. Cleaning of used instrument/equipment/device shall preferably be done in the
CSSD. However, if the same is done in patient care areas, adequate measures are taken for
infection prevention. Cleaning ensures that visible biological material and dirt is
removed. After cleaning the instrument, sets are prepared and packed using the appropriate
material. Shelf life of sterilised items is defined and monitored to increase efficacy.
Spaulding's classification guides the decision to do high/intermediate/low-level
disinfection. Disinfection/sterilisation is performed as per the written guidance. Flash
sterilisation shall only be done in exceptional situations when there is insufficient time
to sterilise an item by the preferred method. The sterilised/disinfected equipment/sets
shall be stored appropriately across the organisation and not just in CSSD. The expiry
date of sterilised instruments/equipment shall be guided by the packing material used and
the mode of sterilisation.

Stop-work check: CORE and starred. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: Issue of an item from CSSD/sterile
store when the sterilisation or disinfection process for that load was not performed as per
written guidance, or when load validation has failed, is a stop-work trigger. The IPC.7
stop-work authority in Section 6 of this policy applies.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Written guidance shall be in consonance with national/international guidelines.
  2. Cleaning shall remove visible biological material and dirt.
  3. Sets shall be prepared and packed using appropriate material after cleaning.
  4. Shelf life of sterilised items shall be defined and monitored.
  5. Spaulding's classification shall guide the decision to do high/intermediate/low-level
     disinfection.
  6. Disinfection/sterilisation shall be performed as per the written guidance.
  7. Flash sterilisation shall only be done in exceptional situations when there is
     insufficient time to sterilise by the preferred method — this is an explicit
     prohibition on routine flash sterilisation.
  8. Sterilised/disinfected equipment/sets shall be stored appropriately across the
     organisation, not only in CSSD.
  9. Expiry date of sterilised items shall be guided by the packing material and mode
     of sterilisation.

Illustrative only:
  — Cleaning preferably done in CSSD: "preferably" — aspirational; if done in patient-
    care areas, adequate IPC measures shall be in place.
  — CDC Guideline for Disinfection and Sterilization 2008 and HISI guidelines: named
    references, informational.

CORE status: Absence of written guidance for the full cleaning-packing-sterilisation-
storage-issue cycle is a CORE gap. Issue without following written guidance is a CORE gap
and stop-work trigger.

Documentation: OE is starred — written guidance covering all steps, shelf-life definition,
Spaulding classification decision table, and load/issue records must be on file.

IPC.7.c
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Reprocessing of instruments, equipment and devices are done as per written guidance.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation identifies instruments, equipment and devices which are suitable for
re-use. The number of re-uses and the process of re-use of these items are defined and
monitored. The patient is informed about the same. The written guidance addresses
cleaning, disinfection or sterilisation between patients. The written guidance shall be
in consonance with the available good practices.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. Issue of a
reprocessed single-use item without a documented, accepted method and risk assessment is
a gap evidenced at assessment; it does not independently trigger the IPC.7 stop-work
section (which is triggered at load validation failure or recall, not reprocessing-status
checks).

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Instruments, equipment and devices suitable for re-use shall be identified.
  2. The number of re-uses for each identified item shall be defined and monitored.
  3. The process of re-use (reprocessing method) shall be defined and monitored.
  4. The patient shall be informed about re-use of items — this is explicitly mandatory
     from the Guidebook.
  5. Written guidance shall address cleaning, disinfection or sterilisation between
     patients for each reprocessed item.
  6. Written guidance shall be in consonance with available good practices.

Illustrative only:
  — No "for example" or "preferable" items in this OE's interpretation beyond what
    is listed above. All elements above carry "shall" force or equivalent.

Note for policy writer: The patient-information requirement is an explicit mandatory
item from the Guidebook ("The patient is informed about the same"). It should appear in
the policy as a requirement, not as an optional step.

Documentation: OE is starred — a documented list of items identified for re-use (with
maximum re-use count), reprocessing method per item, patient information records, and
compliance monitoring records must be on file.

IPC.7.d
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Regular validation tests for sterilisation are carried out and documented.

Guidebook interpretation:
This shall be done by accepted methods, for example bacteriologic, strips, etc.
Physical/chemical tests shall be done daily, and biological tests at least weekly.
Engineering validations like Bowie-Dick tape test and leak rate test need to be carried
out. Each load shall have a unique number and content description. Where applicable,
temperature, pressure and time-record chart shall be maintained. A good reference is
"CDC Guideline for Disinfection and Sterilization in Healthcare Facilities, 2008".

Stop-work check: Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. However, a
failed validation test is the trigger for IPC.7.e (recall procedure) and the IPC.7
stop-work section. A load that fails validation may not be issued; if issue has already
occurred, the recall procedure in IPC.7.e applies.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Physical/chemical tests shall be done daily — the daily minimum is explicit and
     mandatory; do not soften to "regularly".
  2. Biological tests shall be done at least weekly — the weekly minimum is explicit
     and mandatory.
  3. Engineering validations — including Bowie-Dick tape test and leak rate test —
     shall be carried out.
  4. Each load shall have a unique number and content description.
  5. Where applicable, temperature, pressure and time-record chart shall be maintained.

Illustrative only:
  — Bacteriologic, strips: examples of accepted methods ("for example"). The organisation
    uses validated methods; these are examples, not a closed list.
  — CDC Guideline 2008: named reference, informational.

Documentation: OE is starred — load records (unique load number, content description,
physical/chemical indicator results, biological indicator results, temperature/pressure/
time charts as applicable) must be on file for each sterilisation load.

IPC.7.e
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The established recall procedure is implemented when a breakdown in the sterilisation
system is identified.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation shall ensure that the sterilisation procedure is regularly monitored
and in the eventuality of a breakdown it has written guidance for withdrawal of such
items. The organisation could have a batch-processing system with date and machine number
for effective recall. The processes could be verified through a mock drill.

Stop-work check: STOP-WORK TRIGGER: Issue of an instrument, device or pack from sterile
storage when a recall of that load or machine is in effect is a stop-work trigger. The
stop-work authority in Section 6 of this policy applies.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. The sterilisation procedure shall be regularly monitored.
  2. Written guidance for withdrawal (recall) of items when a breakdown is identified
     shall exist.
  3. The recall procedure shall be implemented when a breakdown is identified — both
     the procedure and its implementation are mandatory.

Illustrative only:
  — Batch-processing system with date and machine number: "could have" — aspirational
    mechanism for effective recall; the underlying requirement is the ability to trace
    and withdraw items. A batch system is the recommended approach.
  — Mock drill: "could be verified through a mock drill" — the Guidebook uses "could"
    (aspirational). The policy implementation (method_bodies) treats a mock recall run
    at least annually as a mandatory practice to verify the procedure. The policy draft
    should include this as a mandatory practice; this is a policy enhancement beyond the
    bare Guidebook text.

DISCREPANCY NOTE: Guidebook says "could be verified through a mock drill" (aspirational).
The method_bodies policy implementation states "A mock recall is run at least annually"
(mandatory). The policy draft treats the annual mock drill as mandatory. This is documented
here for traceability.

Documentation: OE is starred — written recall procedure, records of any actual recalls
implemented, and records of at least one annual mock recall exercise must be on file.

IPC.8.a
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation implements occupational health and safety practices as per written
guidance to reduce the risk of transmitting microorganisms among health care providers.

Guidebook interpretation:
Written guidance shall be available on occupational health and safety practices. The
organisation provides adequate resources to its staff for infection prevention and
control. Staff shall adhere to standard precautions. Where appropriate, the organisation
shall have a pre-employment health screening and capture immunisation status. Refer to
IPC.2.c., IPC.3.a., IPC.3.b. and HRM.2.b.

[Note: "_b. HB." at end of Guidebook source is an OCR artefact. Discarded. Cross-refs
to IPC.2.c, IPC.3.a, IPC.3.b, HRM.2.b are informational — in policy draft: "those
requirements are covered in the hospital's other policies."]

Stop-work check: Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. IPC.8 stop-work
triggers are at IPC.8.c (work restrictions) and IPC.8.e (PEP). This OE governs the
written occupational-health framework.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Written guidance shall be available on occupational health and safety practices —
     the OE text requires implementation "as per written guidance"; the guidance must
     exist.
  2. Adequate resources shall be provided to staff for infection prevention and control.
  3. Staff shall adhere to standard precautions — this "shall" is from the Guidebook text;
     it is cross-referenced to IPC.3.a which covers the detail.
  4. Pre-employment health screening and capture of immunisation status: "Where
     appropriate" — this is a qualified mandatory; the organisation defines what is
     appropriate for its staffing model.

Illustrative only:
  — The specific cross-references to IPC.2.c, IPC.3.a, IPC.3.b, and HRM.2.b are
    informational — those requirements are covered in the hospital's other policies.

Documentation: OE is starred — written occupational health and safety guidance (covering
PPE, vaccination access, exposure reporting, work restriction, and pre-employment
screening) must be on file.

IPC.8.b
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation implements an immunisation policy for its staff.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation's immunisation policy for its staff shall be in consonance with the
available evidence. At a minimum, Hepatitis B vaccination shall be provided to staff
involved in direct patient care. Other relevant immunisation is provided as per the risk
from time to time and in accordance with applicable statutory requirements.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no stop-work trigger at this OE.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. The immunisation policy shall be in consonance with the available evidence.
  2. At minimum, Hepatitis B vaccination shall be provided to staff involved in direct
     patient care — the Hepatitis B vaccination floor is explicitly mandatory.
  3. Other relevant immunisation shall be provided as per the risk from time to time and
     in accordance with applicable statutory requirements.

Illustrative only:
  — No "for example" or "preferable" items beyond what is stated above. All elements
    carry mandatory force.

Documentation: OE is starred — written immunisation policy, immunisation register (staff
name, vaccine, dose, date, booster schedule), and evidence of Hepatitis B vaccination
coverage for all direct-patient-care staff must be on file.

Note for policy writer: An unvaccinated direct-care worker without a documented
contraindication or documented refusal is a gap at this starred element.

IPC.8.c
Level: Achievement | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation implements work restrictions for health care providers with transmissible
infections.

Guidebook interpretation:
The restrictions could include limiting role and responsibilities for health care providers
with transmissible infections. The organisation encourages health care providers to report
their illness or exposures and does not penalise them. Examples of transmissible infections
include acute conjunctivitis, chickenpox, acute respiratory infections, smear-positive
case of tuberculosis etc.

Stop-work check: Achievement. STOP-WORK TRIGGER: A healthcare provider continuing
clinical duty when a work restriction for a transmissible infection applies to them is
a stop-work trigger. The colleague who identifies the situation has stop-work authority.
The stop-work authority in Section 6 of this policy applies.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Work restrictions for healthcare providers with transmissible infections shall be
     implemented — the OE text uses "implements", carrying mandatory force.
  2. The organisation shall encourage healthcare providers to report illness or exposures
     and shall not penalise them for reporting.

Illustrative only:
  — "Could include limiting role and responsibilities" — describes how restrictions may
    be structured; the form is for the organisation to determine.
  — Acute conjunctivitis, chickenpox, acute respiratory infections, smear-positive TB:
    examples of transmissible infections ("Examples of transmissible infections include
    ... etc.") — not a closed list; the Guidebook uses "etc." signalling the list is
    open.

Achievement level note: Evidence of restrictions that were actually applied (not just
a policy document), and a culture of reporting without fear of penalty, is what
assessors look for. A policy statement alone is insufficient for this Achievement OE.

IPC.8.d
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation implements measures for blood and body fluid exposure prevention.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation ensures that health care providers use appropriate personnel protective
equipment to prevent blood and body fluid exposures. The organisation shall strive to
eliminate the use of needle devices whenever safe and effective alternatives are available.
It could consider providing needle devices with safety features.

Stop-work check: Commitment — no standalone stop-work trigger at this OE. IPC.8.e (PEP)
is the stop-work trigger for the post-exposure event. This OE governs prevention of
exposure. From method_bodies, a recapping culture on a unit is treated as an incident;
this is the enforcement mechanism for the prevention obligation.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. Healthcare providers shall use appropriate PPE to prevent blood and body fluid
     exposures.
  2. The organisation shall strive to eliminate the use of needle devices whenever safe
     and effective alternatives are available — "shall strive" carries a strong
     aspiration approaching mandatory obligation.

Illustrative only:
  — Needle devices with safety features: "could consider" — aspirational.

Note for policy writer: From the method_bodies, the practice of sharps containers being
at the point of use and replaced before they are full is a standard policy practice
implementation for this OE, going beyond the bare Guidebook text. Include it in the policy.

Documentation: No star — IPC round records confirming PPE availability and safe-sharps
practices (including no-recapping observation records) are the required evidence.

IPC.8.e
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Appropriate post-exposure prophylaxis is provided to all staff members concerned.

Guidebook interpretation:
The organisation provides post-exposure prophylaxis for hepatitis B and HIV exposure.
This shall align with National and/or International guidelines. Infection prevention and
control Nurse maintains documentation of post-exposure prophylaxis records. For example,
PEP for needle stick injury.

Stop-work check: STOP-WORK TRIGGER: Leaving a blood or body-fluid exposure (including
a needle-stick) without starting the organisation's post-exposure path the same shift is
a stop-work trigger. The stop-work authority in Section 6 of this policy applies.

Mandatory (shall):
  1. PEP for hepatitis B exposure shall be provided — the hepatitis B PEP is explicitly
     named as mandatory.
  2. PEP for HIV exposure shall be provided — the HIV PEP is explicitly named as
     mandatory.
  3. PEP shall align with national/international guidelines.
  4. The IPC Nurse shall maintain documentation of PEP records.

Illustrative only:
  — PEP for needle-stick injury: example of a qualifying exposure event ("For example");
    not the only type of exposure requiring PEP.

Documentation: OE is starred — written PEP protocol aligned with national/international
guidelines, and a PEP record file maintained by the IPC Nurse (with the staff member's
name, date, type of exposure, PEP given, follow-up) must be on file. Confidentiality of
the record follows the hospital's patient/staff confidentiality policy.

STOP-WORK — IPC.7 (Standard 7: Sterilisation and Disinfection of Instruments,
             Equipment and Devices)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do not issue an instrument, device or pack from sterile storage when validation of
that sterilisation load has failed, or when a recall of that load or machine is in
effect.

Stop-work applies to issue from CSSD / sterile store. Immediate life-saving use of
the only available item is documented and the CSSD In-Charge is told the same shift.

The person who stops tells the CSSD In-Charge and the Infection Prevention and Control
Officer the same shift. Refusing to issue a failed or recalled load is not a
disciplinary matter.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STOP-WORK — IPC.8 (Standard 8: Prevention of Healthcare Associated Infections in Staff)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Do not continue clinical duty when a work restriction for a transmissible infection
applies to you.

Do not leave a blood or body-fluid exposure (including a needle-stick) without starting
the organisation's post-exposure path the same shift.

Stop-work applies to the restricted duty and to delay of PEP. Immediate first aid at
the exposure site starts at once.

The person who stops (or the colleague who sees the restriction being ignored) tells
the Occupational Health Physician and the Infection Prevention and Control Officer the
same shift. Reporting an exposure or observing a restriction is not a disciplinary matter.
