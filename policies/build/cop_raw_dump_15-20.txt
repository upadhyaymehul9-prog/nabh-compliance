COP.15 — Organ Transplant Programme
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Standard text (verbatim):
The organ transplant programme is carried out safely.

OE count: 4 | COREs: a, d | Stars: b★
Levels: a CORE, b–c Commitment, d CORE | Stop-work: NO (see statutory note above)

Doc no: HCO/COP/POL/15

SCOPE NOTE: This standard applies only to organisations that provide organ
transplantation services. COP.15.d — organ donation awareness — is the sole OE
that applies to ALL organisations irrespective of whether transplantation is performed.
State this conditional scope clearly at the outset of the policy.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COP.15.a
Level: CORE | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organ transplant programme shall be in consonance with the legal requirements
and shall be conducted ethically.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 135):
The organisation shall ensure that the required regulatory licences are in place and
adhere to the requirements of 'The Transplantation of Human Organs Act'. The
organisation also ensures that the relevant medical professionals have been
permitted by the appropriate authorities to participate in the organ transplant
programme. The organisation shall have a designated transplant coordinator. The
organisation shall ensure that the requisite reports are submitted to the appropriate
authorities as per the defined timeframe.

Stop-work check: CORE — formal stop-work flag not assigned in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS.
However: operating without valid regulatory licences under the Transplantation of
Human Organs Act is a statutory violation. Any transplant activity conducted without
required licences or without permitted professionals is an immediate statutory
stop-work condition. This is a legal floor, not merely an accreditation requirement.

STATUTE: Transplantation of Human Organs Act (named verbatim in Guidebook). All
requirements of this Act, as amended, are binding. The policy disclaimer must
explicitly name this statute and state that the hospital is responsible for verifying
current statutory requirements.

FIVE MANDATORY REQUIREMENTS — preserve all five:
  1. Required regulatory licences shall be in place (shall).
  2. Organisation shall adhere to the Transplantation of Human Organs Act (shall —
     statutory obligation).
  3. Relevant medical professionals shall have been permitted by appropriate
     authorities to participate in the programme (shall — professional authorisation
     is a pre-condition, not a post-hoc step).
  4. Organisation shall have a designated transplant coordinator (shall — the
     designation is mandatory, not advisory).
  5. Requisite reports shall be submitted to appropriate authorities as per defined
     timeframe (shall — both the submission and the timeframe are mandatory; the
     timeframe is set by the Act, not by the organisation).

Mandatory (shall):
  — All five requirements above (shall — CORE standard; non-compliance is not
    acceptable for accreditation).

Non-mandatory:
  — The specific form of the transplant coordinator role (full-time vs. designated
    from existing staff) is the organisation's determination within statutory limits.

DRAFTING NOTE: This OE is CORE. Unlike starred Commitment OEs, CORE failure is
a direct accreditation disqualifier. The statutory angle makes it doubly non-negotiable.
Do not frame this as a "best practice" element. State it as an absolute requirement
with explicit reference to the Transplantation of Human Organs Act (without reproducing
its text).

COP.15.b
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
Care of transplant patients is guided by clinical practice guidelines.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 135):
Written guidance based on standard treatment guidelines/sound clinical practices
govern the care of transplant patients, which include an indication for transplant,
recipient fitness, donor fitness, education and consent process, organ recovery and
ensuring organ compatibility. The organ transplant programme has documented
organ-specific clinical practice guidelines for the pre-transplant, transplant, and
discharge phase of transplantation. Individualised care plans are developed to guide
the care of transplant patients. It is preferable that the organisation encourages
departments to develop care pathways, which help guide these patients. A
multidisciplinary team comprising of nurses, dietician, rehabilitation services,
psychologist and/or medical social worker and infection prevention and control
specialist is in place. It also ensures availability of other resources such as
consumables, pharmaceutical supplies and other diagnostic requirements specifically
required for transplant.

Stop-work check: Not in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. Absence of written clinical-practice
guidance is a compliance gap for this starred Commitment OE, but does not fire a
formal stop-work trigger in the accreditation framework.

SIX MANDATORY CONTENT AREAS OF TRANSPLANT CLINICAL GUIDANCE — preserve all six:
  1. Indication for transplant
  2. Recipient fitness
  3. Donor fitness
  4. Education and consent process
  5. Organ recovery
  6. Ensuring organ compatibility

THREE MANDATORY PHASE-SPECIFIC CPG REQUIREMENTS:
  1. Pre-transplant phase CPGs
  2. Transplant phase CPGs
  3. Discharge phase CPGs
  — All three phases require documented organ-specific CPGs (shall).

Mandatory (shall):
  — Written guidance shall be based on STG/sound clinical practices (shall).
  — Written guidance shall include all six content areas above (shall — stated inclusions).
  — Organ-specific CPGs shall be documented for all three phases (shall — pre-transplant,
    transplant, discharge; all three are stated).
  — Individualised care plans shall be developed for transplant patients (shall —
    "are developed" in the Guidebook; care-plan development is mandatory).
  — An MDT shall be in place comprising nurses, dietician, rehabilitation services,
    psychologist and/or medical social worker, and IPC specialist (shall — the
    multi-disciplinary composition is stated as a requirement).
  — Resources for transplant shall be available: consumables, pharmaceutical supplies,
    diagnostic requirements specifically required for transplant (shall).

Non-mandatory:
  — Care pathways: the Guidebook says the organisation "encourages departments to
    develop care pathways" — this is advisory at the level of internal promotion;
    care pathways themselves are "preferable" and not a shall for this OE.
  — Whether the transplant coordinator also serves on the MDT is the organisation's
    determination.

Documentation: This OE is starred — the written clinical-practice guidance document
(including organ-specific CPGs and care plans) is the required documentation.

DRAFTING NOTE: The six content areas are stated inclusions, not examples. Do not
reduce to "transplant guidelines generally." All six must be addressed in the
written guidance.

COP.15.c
Level: Commitment | No star

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation ensures education and counselling of recipient and donor through
trained / qualified counsellors before organ transplantation.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 135–136):
The organisation ensures that qualified/trained counsellors are available to counsel
the recipient and donor about the proposed organ donation. Doctors and counsellors
educate and counsel the recipient and donor on the benefits and possible risks to
make informed decisions. The recipients are also educated about immunosuppression
(benefits and risks) and the required monitoring and follow up. Counselling can also
be done during the interview by the Local Authorisation Committee of the organisation.
Evidence of counselling is recorded and maintained in the requisite statutory formats
apart from the organisation's individual needs.

Stop-work check: Not in STOP_WORK_PROPOSALS. However, the Guidebook's requirement
that counselling be done BEFORE organ transplantation has a clear sequence obligation —
transplantation proceeding without completed counselling of recipient and donor would
be a clinical governance failure.

MANDATORY CONTENT AREAS OF COUNSELLING — preserve all:
  1. Benefits of organ donation/transplantation (for both recipient and donor)
  2. Possible risks of organ donation/transplantation (for both recipient and donor)
  3. Immunosuppression — benefits and risks (for recipients)
  4. Required monitoring and follow-up (for recipients)

Mandatory (shall):
  — Qualified/trained counsellors shall be available to counsel recipient and donor
    (shall — "qualified/trained" is a double qualifier; the counsellor must satisfy both).
  — Counselling shall cover the four content areas above (shall — all four stated).
  — Counselling shall be done BEFORE organ transplantation (shall — timing is
    explicit and is part of the OE text).
  — Evidence of counselling shall be recorded and maintained in requisite statutory
    formats AND the organisation's individual record formats (shall — both the
    statutory format and the organisation's own format are required).

Non-mandatory:
  — Counselling during the Local Authorisation Committee interview is explicitly
    stated as an acceptable modality — this is a permissive statement, not a
    requirement that the LAC interview always be used.
  — Whether counselling is done by doctors, dedicated counsellors, or a combination
    is the organisation's determination — "qualified/trained" is the standard.

DRAFTING NOTE: The BEFORE TRANSPLANTATION timing is part of the OE text itself.
It cannot be softened or left implied. State it explicitly in the policy procedure.

The organisation shall take measures to create awareness regarding organ donation.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 136):
This would be applicable for all organisations irrespective of whether organ
transplantation is carried out or not. This could be in the form of standees,
hand-outs, etc. The myths of the public regarding organ donation shall be addressed.
Whenever a patient is declared brain dead, the family are also counselled on organ

Stop-work check: CORE — formal stop-work not assigned. Absence of any awareness
measures is a CORE compliance gap. No real-time stop-work trigger from individual
awareness activities.

UNIVERSAL APPLICABILITY NOTE: This OE applies to ALL hospitals, including those
that do NOT provide organ transplantation services. Even if the rest of COP.15 is
not in scope, COP.15.d is. State this explicitly in the policy scope section.

BRAIN-DEAD PATIENT PROTOCOL — preserve exact language:
  — Whenever a patient is declared brain dead: family shall be counselled on organ
    transplant WHERE APPROPRIATE (shall — "where appropriate" is a clinical judgment;
    the counselling obligation fires when declared brain dead AND when it is clinically
    appropriate; it is not waived on personal discretion).
  — Counselling shall be done in a very sensitive and courteous manner (shall —
    both "sensitive" and "courteous" are stated attributes of the engagement).

TWO MANDATORY COMPONENTS of the awareness obligation:
  1. Measures to create general public awareness of organ donation
  2. Brain-dead patient family counselling (when declared brain dead, where appropriate)

Mandatory (shall):
  — Organisation shall take measures to create organ donation awareness (shall —
    the obligation is to take measures, not to achieve a particular outcome;
    measures must exist and be implemented).
  — Myths of the public regarding organ donation shall be addressed (shall —
    the myth-addressing content is a stated requirement, not advisory).
  — Family of brain-dead patients shall be counselled on organ transplant where
    appropriate (shall — conditional but mandatory when triggered).
  — Brain-dead family counselling shall be done in a very sensitive and courteous
    manner (shall — manner of engagement is mandatory, not optional).

Non-mandatory:
  — Specific awareness media (standees, hand-outs) are illustrative — the
    organisation determines which formats are appropriate.
  — Who conducts awareness activities (clinical, non-clinical, external) is
    the organisation's determination.

DRAFTING NOTE: This OE is CORE and universally applicable. Do not bury it in a
scope section that implies it applies only to transplant hospitals. Write a distinct
sub-section for brain-dead patient family counselling and address the sensitive
manner of engagement explicitly.

STOP-WORK PROPOSALS entry (internal):
  16: "High-risk care (falls/pressure/DVT/restraints/vulnerable) without identification

stop_work_text(16) — complete hospital-facing text:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Do not leave a patient identified as vulnerable, at fall risk, at pressure-ulcer risk,
at DVT risk, or needing restraints without the precautions and monitoring required
by the organisation's written guidance.

Do not apply or continue restraints without a documented clinical order and ongoing
review.

Stop-work applies to missing precautions / unauthorised restraint — not to emergency
physical intervention needed to prevent immediate harm, which is followed at once by
documentation and medical review.

The person who stops tells the «treating doctor» and the «Ward / ICU In-Charge» the
same shift. Refusing to leave high-risk patients unprotected is not a disciplinary
matter.

COP.16.a
Level: CORE | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation identifies and manages vulnerable patients.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 136–137):
Written guidance for identification and management of vulnerable patients is developed
in consonance with statutory requirements, national and international guidelines.

It shall include (but not limited to) elderly, children, differently-abled and/or
mentally challenged, patient with risk of suicide, self-harm, comatose, critically ill,
patients under sedation and anaesthesia, pregnant women, patients on dialysis, patients
receiving chemotherapy, immunosuppressed patients, etc. The guidance shall state who
is responsible for identifying these patients, risk management in these patients and
monitoring of these patients (at least twice a day).

The guidance shall include how informed consent is obtained from a vulnerable patient,
and from the family or legal representative of a patient incapable of making an
independent decision.

Refer to PRE.4.d.
The care is organised and delivered in accordance with written guidance.
Refer to the glossary for a definition of "vulnerable patient".

Stop-work check: YES — COP.16 stop-work. Leaving an identified vulnerable patient
without required precautions and monitoring is a direct stop-work trigger.

statutory requirements — the specific statutes depend on the patient category
(e.g., Mental Healthcare Act for patients at risk of suicide/self-harm, relevant
child protection legislation for children). The Guidebook does not name specific acts
but the statutory consonance obligation is stated; the hospital must verify applicable
statutes.

MANDATORY MONITORING FREQUENCY — preserve verbatim:
  — "at least twice a day" — monitoring of vulnerable patients shall occur at least
    twice a day. Do not soften to "regularly" or "periodically."

ILLUSTRATIVE VULNERABLE PATIENT CATEGORIES (not an exhaustive mandatory list —
"but not limited to" is stated):
  Elderly, children, differently-abled and/or mentally challenged, patients at risk of
  suicide, patients at risk of self-harm, comatose patients, critically ill patients,

FOUR MANDATORY WRITTEN-GUIDANCE CONTENT AREAS — preserve all four:

  2. Who is responsible for identifying vulnerable patients
  3. Risk management for identified vulnerable patients

COP.16.e
Level: Commitment | Star ★ (documentation required)

Verbatim OE text:
The organisation identifies and manages patients who need restraints.

Guidebook interpretation (PDF p. 137):
Restraints include physical and chemical restraints. Written guidance is used to
identify patients who need restraints and provide care to them. The organisation may
minimise the use of restraints wherever possible keeping in view patient safety.
The written guidance shall incorporate the suggested situations where restraints could
be used. It shall also specify as to who can authorise the use of restraints, the
frequency of monitoring these patients and the validity of restraint orders. The need
for restraints is regularly reassessed, and the least invasive restraint is selected
if required. The rationale for using restraints is explained to the family, and consent
is obtained where appropriate (as directed by statutory requirements). These patients
shall be monitored more frequently for the complications that the restraints may
cause. For example, neurovascular deficit. When restraints are used, the following
shall be documented in the medical record: the reason for using restraints and the
time frame during which restraints were used.

Stop-work check: YES — COP.16 stop-work. Applying or continuing restraints without
a documented clinical order, or without ongoing review, is a direct stop-work trigger.

SCOPE — restraint types (both are covered):
  — Physical restraints
  — Chemical restraints
  Both must be addressed in the written guidance.

EIGHT MANDATORY WRITTEN-GUIDANCE CONTENT AREAS — preserve all eight:
  1. Identification criteria — which patients need restraints
  2. Care provision for restrained patients
  3. Suggested situations where restraints could be used
  4. Who can authorise the use of restraints
  5. Frequency of monitoring restrained patients
  6. Validity of restraint orders

   DNR/DNI/AND decisions are made at clinical discretion independent of the legal
   framework.

4. STATUTORY DISCLAIMER (COP.20.c): Name the relevant legal instruments — ICMR DNR
   Guideline and Supreme Court directives 2023 — in the policy disclaimer. State
   that the hospital is responsible for verifying current and State-specific requirements.

5. FIVE NEED DIMENSIONS (COP.20.d): psychological, emotional, cultural, religious,
   socio-cultural — all five stated. Do not collapse to "cultural and spiritual."
   "Socio-cultural beliefs" is a separate stated dimension.

6. BOTH VERBS (COP.20.d): "addressed and respected" — both are mandatory. Addressed
   means identified and responded to; respected means the patient/family's framework
   governs the care response. A policy that only identifies needs without requiring a
   response does not satisfy this OE.

7. STAFF EDUCATION (COP.20.d): A separate mandatory obligation — include a training
   component for end-of-life unique needs in the COP.20 policy's training section.

8. PATIENT/FAMILY INVOLVEMENT — "ALL SUCH DECISIONS" (COP.20.e): No exceptions are
   stated. The involvement requirement applies to all symptomatic and comfort-care
   decisions. Do not introduce exceptions beyond what the "possible" and "as per law"
   qualifiers already provide.

9. "AUTOPSY AND ORGAN DONATION" (COP.20.a, content area 2): These are sensitive
   topics explicitly named. The policy must require that staff handle these conversations
   in a sensitive manner. Cross-reference COP.15.d (brain-dead patient family counselling)
   to maintain consistency.
